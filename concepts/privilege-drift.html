Privilege Drift / Scope Sprawl

The gradual, seemingly-reasonable accumulation of an AI agent's permissions beyond its original scope and purpose over time — each individual grant justified in isolation, but the aggregate expanding the attack surface and violating least privilege; amplified when multiple agents share resources in a multi-agent system.

Also known as: privilege drift, scope sprawl, permission accumulation, over-permissioning, permission creep

Part of: Agent Governance (Internal)

    Because enterprise AI agents infer whether they're operating under normal, incident, or recovery conditions from prompts and accumulated context rather than receiving it as authoritative input, temporary emergency exceptions quietly harden into permanent runtime behavior ('exception drift') — the fix is to make organizational operating mode a first-class, scoped, time-bound runtime construct, injected alongside identity and permissions, whose closure the platform itself can verify rather than merely document.
  • What Happens After Every Company Becomes a Data Company? Art Anthony · 2026-07-28 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    As every company becomes a data company and AI agents begin consuming that data autonomously, organizations must abandon convenience-first identity patterns — broad SSO, single super-profiles, session-long OAuth grants — in favor of a Zero-Trust posture of least-privilege, just-in-time, minimally-scoped, continuously-evaluated access for humans and agents alike, treating themselves as active data stewards rather than passive collectors.
  • 5 Signs That Authenticated API Traffic Is Actually Malicious J Simpson · 2026-07-21 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Because most API attacks now come through fully authenticated sessions rather than broken authentication, and valid credentials make an attacker's requests indistinguishable from legitimate traffic at the authentication layer, API security must shift from gating access at login to continuously profiling authenticated users' behavior and flagging deviations — unusual resource requests, access patterns, request volume, bulk actions, and error rates — since these behavioral signals, not credentials, are where malicious authenticated traffic actually reveals itself.
  • How to Manage Privilege Drift in Multi-Agent Systems Janet Wagner · 2026-07-14 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Privilege drift — the incremental, individually-reasonable accumulation of an AI agent's permissions beyond its original purpose — multiplies across the agents, shared resources, and communication channels of a multi-agent system into an attack surface no single decision ever consciously approved, and it can only be managed (never eliminated) through continuous cross-agent inventory, runtime-enforced least privilege, just-in-time access, and behavioral monitoring.
