Independent-First Group Elicitation

Collecting each person's view, ranking or vote independently, in writing and before discussion, then using the meeting only to discuss disagreement and deciding outside the room, because opinions are contagious and the most confident or senior voice otherwise dominates.

Also known as: nominal group, discover, discuss, decide, forced rank, opinion contagion, independent written opinions, dispersion of opinions, vote forum

    Because knowing about our biases does not stop us from acting on them, better decisions come from structure rather than awareness: gathering opinions independently before any discussion, deciding in advance (via premortems and kill criteria) what signals will make us stop, and judging decisions apart from their outcomes.
  • This will make you a better decision maker | Annie Duke (Thinking In Bets, former pro poker player) Lenny Rachitsky (Lenny's Podcast) — guest Annie Duke · 2024-05-02 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Decision quality improves less by learning about biases than by changing the procedure around the decision: make the implicit explicit (written, rubric-based, forecast-based judgments), gather each person's opinion independently before the meeting, reserve the meeting for discussing disagreement, decide outside the room, shorten feedback loops by tracking intermediate signals, and pre-commit to kill criteria — because intuition and group dynamics are unreliable in ways you cannot detect unless the judgment is externalised and checkable.
