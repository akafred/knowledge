Organizational Coherence

The contingent alignment among an organization's genuinely separate structures (goals, teaming, ownership, technology, incentives) that must be continually re-established as the organization changes — distinct from legibility (visibility) and organizational debt (accumulated liability); a property to measure and maintain rather than architect once.

Also known as: coherence, organizational coherence, spacetime crystal, spacetime crystals, coherence ceiling, business gardening, structural coherence, API coherence

  • TBM 423: Why Defining Teams Is So Hard John Cutler · 2026-05-21 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Defining teams in product/tech orgs is hard not because the analytic skill is rare, but because honestly naming the gap between the aspirational org chart and how work actually flows threatens existing power, incentives, and narratives — so the work is political and emotional, not technical, and the goal should be to hold both views at once and act on the gap rather than collapse it.
  • TBM 423: Why Defining Teams Is So Hard John Cutler from The Beautiful Mess · 2026-05-21 Reader 🖥️
    Honestly describing how teams actually work in product-and-technology organizations is cognitively easy but politically expensive, because the gap between the legible aspirational org chart and the lived collaboration reality is sustained by asymmetric incentives (cheap for product to redefine, costly for engineering to absorb, invisible to design) rather than by any lack of skill or insight — so the real organizational competence is the willingness to hold both views at once and work with the gap.
