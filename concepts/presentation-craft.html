Presentation & Public Speaking Craft

The craft and decision-making around presenting ideas to an audience — when a live presentation, recording, or written document is the right medium, and how to execute whichever is chosen well.

    A 'presentation' properly means the live, presenter-orchestrated act of storytelling, not the slide deck, so before building one you should work up a hierarchy of cheaper alternatives — document, infodeck, interactive web app, recorded audio/video — and reserve an actual (especially live) presentation for the rare cases that genuinely require synchronous human interaction.

  • explicitRecorded audio or video is usually a better default than a live presentation, because the audience controls pace (speed up/slow down/replay), gets captions and chapter markers, and the act of recording forces the presenter to cut fluff.
    Do you even need a presentation? · p6
  • explicitA live presentation should be reserved for situations that specifically require synchronous human interaction — real-time influence, fielding objections, networking, energising a community — because it is the costliest option: it demands scheduling overhead and heavy preparation, and (unlike a recording) the audience cannot control its pace.
    Do you even need a presentation? · p8
  • implicitCommunication media form an implicit cost/overhead hierarchy — document, infodeck, interactive web app, recording, live presentation, in ascending order of production and audience-attendance cost — and the rational choice is the cheapest rung that still achieves the communicative goal.
    Do you even need a presentation? · p9
  • implicitOrganizations and individuals default to over-using slide decks and live presentations for information that would be better served by writing — otherwise there would be no need to write an article arguing readers should ask 'do you even need a presentation?'
