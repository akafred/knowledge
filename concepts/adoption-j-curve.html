Adoption J-Curve (Instability and Verification Tax)

The pattern where a new technology's early quick wins are followed by a trough as the hard work arrives, then compounding gains. In AI adoption the trough is made of delivery instability and the verification burden from low trust.

Also known as: J-curve, J-curve of transformation, productivity dip, instability tax, verification tax

    AI is an amplifier of whatever organizational system it lands in, so its returns come not from adopting the tools but from fixing the surrounding system (seven DORA capabilities, small batches, user focus, platforms that serve agents and non-engineer builders) and even from radically rethinking bottlenecks like code review, while treating DORA's findings only as hypotheses to test locally.
  • Are You Getting Your Money's Worth from AI? James Shore · 2026-09-08 Reader 🖥️
    Because AI's costs and benefits in software development are real, substantial, and easily misjudged by anecdote, self-assessment, or gut feel, organizations facing consequential tradeoffs between speed, cost, and people's jobs must rigorously measure both sides — using durable, outcome-anchored, approach-level metrics rather than gameable activity proxies like lines of code, pull-request count, or individual performance targets.
  • How to measure AI developer productivity in 2025 | Nicole Forsgren Lenny's Podcast (Lenny Rachitsky interviewing Nicole Forsgren) · 2025-10-19 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Because lines-of-code-style productivity metrics are trivially gamed by verbose LLM output and the DORA/SPACE frameworks were not designed for AI's new failure modes (earlier and faster feedback loops, nondeterministic output requiring trust verification, review time displacing writing time), measuring whether AI is actually helping developers requires starting with qualitative listening and short forced-choice surveys, adding trust as an explicit new dimension, and framing whatever is measured in the specific business terms (market share, margin, velocity) that an organization's leadership already uses -- while openly acknowledging that AI's contribution usually can't be cleanly separated from concurrent developer-experience improvements.
