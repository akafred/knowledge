System One / Non-Autoregressive Decision Models

A proposed class of AI models that abandon autoregressive string generation entirely, instead emitting typed, calibrated probabilistic decisions in parallel over a fixed schema — marketed via a System 1/System 2 (fast/intuitive vs. slow/deliberate) framing borrowed from Kahneman's dual-process theory — as a fast/cheap complement to LLMs for classification, routing, and scoring embedded in software; distinct from diffusion text models (still generates strings) and from ordinary structured-output LLM wrappers (still autoregressive underneath).

Also known as: System One Models, System 1 model, System 1 / Calibrated Decision Models, Jev, RLCD, Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions, typed decision model, typed decision models, calibrated decision model, non-autoregressive decision models, calibrated probabilistic decisions, function-call intelligence

Part of: Inference Optimization

  • explicitJev's claimed speed/efficiency advantage stems from two design choices: RLCD training toward structured, non-generative outputs, and parallel processing of independent questions within a single request rather than LLMs' sequential token-by-token generation.

  • explicitJev is trained with a new method, Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions (RLCD), which optimizes for calibrated, epistemically honest probabilities on System One tasks, distinct from RLHF (human preference) and RLVR (verifiable rewards).

  • implicitBranding the product line "System One Models" while claiming they are engineered to be more reliable than the alternative inverts the usual reliability connotation of the Kahneman metaphor it borrows, since in Kahneman's own framework System 1 is characteristically the faster but more error-prone of the two modes.
    Introducing System One Models & Jev · p10
  • explicitThe company scopes its own use-case boundary: System One Models are pitched for embedded AI-powered workflows (classify, route, score, extract, branch), map-reduce over large datasets, real-time applications, and verifying/guardrailing LLM outputs — not for human-in-the-loop chat/copilot tasks, demos, or open-ended verifiable-by-execution problems, which it concedes remain LLM territory.
