Cross-Vendor Protocol Standardization

The pattern by which rival AI vendors donate protocols they compete around (MCP, A2A, AGENTS.md) to neutral, multi-stakeholder foundations for shared governance — so that independent implementation by competitors, not any single company's roadmap, is what turns an interface into durable infrastructure, following the historical arc of Ethernet and the Language Server Protocol.

Also known as: neutral governance, agentic ai foundation, protocol donation, standards body (ai protocols), de facto standard (ai protocol), independent implementation, tcp/ip of the federated enterprise

Open gaps (2)

  • incompleteThe closing wager — 'the interfaces are likely to last' — is stated with confidence but the essay never specifies what would falsify it: a major founding vendor forking MCP, a rival protocol displacing it, or one founding company (e.g. Anthropic, as MCP's originator and steward) accumulating outsized influence inside the supposedly neutral Agentic AI Foundation. Without a falsification condition, the durability claim is unfalsifiable as stated.
    The Interfaces Are Arriving — read next: Track the Agentic AI Foundation's actual governance structure and voting/veto rights among Anthropic, Block, OpenAI, and the supporting companies (Google, Microsoft, AWS, Cloudflare, Bloomberg) over the next 1-2 years, and watch for defections or competing forks.
  • incompleteThe essay frames the Agentic AI Foundation's governance as already 'neutral' by virtue of its multi-stakeholder cofounder list, but never examines the internal power dynamics of that governance — whether Anthropic, as MCP's originator and the party that authored the spec revisions cited in the piece, retains asymmetric practical influence over a nominally neutral process. This mirrors a live skepticism elsewhere in the corpus about treating a young, multi-stakeholder standards body as already settled or disinterested.
