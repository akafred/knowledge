Jevons Paradox

When technology makes a resource cheaper, total consumption rises — applied to cognition and labor to argue AI produces more work and more engineers, not fewer.

Also known as: Jevons paradox, Jevons' Paradox, Jevons paradox (Wardley's reading), Jevons paradox (on cognition), demand elasticity, unhobbling

Original (1850s–1860s, William Stanley Jevons): when coal extraction became 10x cheaper, net spending on coal rose, because at the lower price entirely new uses became economically viable. Shoup's application: cognition is now near-free; therefore tasks that were not economically viable as cognitive work will now be done, and the number of engineers (and jobs) will rise, not fall.
