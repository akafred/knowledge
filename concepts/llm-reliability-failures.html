LLM Reliability Failures

Concrete, production-relevant failure modes — fabricated output delivered with confidence, and systematic blind spots inherited from training data that persist even under self-reflection.

Also known as: hallucination, training biases

  • The habit that makes AI worth using Ernie Svenson · 2026-09-11 Reader 🖥️
    The value AI adds to information-checking is entirely gated by a human habit — noticing when a claim is surprising or consequential and immediately submitting it to an AI chatbot for source-backed validation before believing or repeating it — because AI has made verification nearly effortless without making people any more inclined to do it.
  • When Guardrails Go Wrong Mike Loukides · 2026-08-19 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Because no classifier can eliminate both false positives and false negatives at once, AI guardrails that are overly broad, whole-conversation-scoped, and opaque about their own boundaries make models less useful without making them meaningfully safer, so labs should deliberately re-balance safety classifiers toward fewer false positives rather than defaulting to maximal caution.
  • Presentation: From Thousands to One: Building LLM-Powered Selection Systems Jendrik Jördening · 2026-08-17 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    When you need an LLM to select one item from among many database-backed options (an ID, a station, a record), you must strip the model down to only the irreducibly textual/semantic step it is actually good at — schema-restricted, range-bounded, and independently validated by a second model call — while pushing every deterministic operation (filtering, comparison, control flow) into ordinary code, recovering a classic model-view-controller separation that a single freeform LLM call destroys.
  • The Economics of Agentic AI: Engineering for Imperfection Artur Huk · 2026-07-24 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    The runaway cost and catastrophic security failures of agentic AI both stem from treating a missing structural constraint (context, authority, evidence, or temporal validity) as a reasoning problem to be solved with more model intelligence, so enterprises must instead wrap probabilistic models in a deterministic governance 'airlock' — syntactic, semantic, and temporal gates that treat model output as an unprivileged claim — which converts model accuracy from a safety prerequisite into a tunable economic variable.
  • Inside a PhD Student's Research Workflow | Studying Parasocial Relationships with Alvina Lai Consensus · 2026-06-25 Reader 🖥️
    Effective AI-assisted literature research, as Alvina Lai practices it with Consensus, is not a single search step but a repeatable staged workflow — natural-language topic search over a peer-reviewed corpus, triage of algorithmically-flagged key papers, source capture into a library, verification of drafted claims against extracted evidence, a second targeted search to establish a literature gap, and (newly) citation-graph-based discovery — that replaces exact-keyword, paper-by-paper verification with faster, terminology-agnostic retrieval and writing-stage claim-checking.
  • GitHub Copilot CLI combines model families for a second opinion Nick McKenna · 2026-02 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    A single coding agent reviewing its own work is limited by its training biases; pairing it with a second model from a different AI family at key checkpoints closes most of the quality gap to a stronger model, catching architectural errors, subtle bugs, and cross-file conflicts that self-reflection alone misses.
