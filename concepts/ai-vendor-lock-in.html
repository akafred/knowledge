AI Vendor/Cost Lock-In

The customer-side operational risk of being financially/technically trapped by AI vendor or token dependence, such that a price increase forces a choice between a much larger bill and cutting AI use, headcount, or capability.

Also known as: lock-in, vendor lock-in (AI), token cost lock-in, AI dependency lock-in, switching cost (AI)

  • GitLab Duo Expands Self-Hosted AI Options Through Microsoft Foundry Craig Risi · 2026-09-22 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    GitLab's Microsoft Foundry integration shows that enterprise AI development tooling is decoupling into two separable layers — a vendor-neutral development/DevSecOps control layer and a swappable, self-hosted model/infrastructure layer chosen for data sovereignty and compliance — rather than requiring a single, centrally managed AI provider.
  • Measuring AI's Unintended Consequences James Shore · 2026-09-22 Reader 🖥️
    Because AI's unintended consequences — declining maintainability, burnout, and vendor/token lock-in — carry real economic costs but, unlike delivery speed, cannot be measured by a controlled trial and instead surface only gradually and noisily, organizations should track a cluster of imperfect lagging indicators (chiefly value-add percentage, backed by DORA metrics and token usage) and build explicit long-horizon scenario models (a maintenance-cost 'tax,' an excess-code-churn model, and a lock-in variant of both) to inform — never replace — judgment, even though the most trusted of these indicators is itself unusually easy to game.
