Explore / Expand / Extract

Beck's company-lifecycle phase model — find what works, grow past bottlenecks, optimize margins — each phase demanding incompatible behaviors.

Also known as: 3X (Explore/Expand/Extract), 3X, explore / expand / extract (3 X's), explore/expand/extract

Documents (5)

  • Reject Change, Sometimes Kent Beck · 2026-09-02 Reader 🖥️
    Because a growing product's payoff structure differs by 3X phase — roughly symmetric win/loss during Extract, sharply upside-skewed during Explore, and ruin-inclusive (win/die/switch) during Expand — the correct exposure to volatility differs sharply too: Extract calls for the disciplined, rebalancing 'Shannon's Demon' strategy of protecting revenue and keeping changes reversible even at some cost to upside (i.e., deliberately rejecting some change), Explore calls for going all in on volatility despite that same strategy's mathematical dominance in symmetric bets, and Expand calls for a third move — investing directly to reduce the probability of catastrophic failure — so 'embrace change' is not a universal software-development virtue but a phase-specific one.
  • Canon 3X: Explore/Expand/Extract Kent Beck · 2026-07-30 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Because a growing idea passes through three phases — Explore, Expand, Extract — that each demand categorically different, even incompatible approaches to finance, team size, strategy, personnel, and risk, applying one phase's playbook to a project actually in a different phase kills the idea; managing a portfolio well means correctly matching each project's approach to its actual phase, and prioritizing Expand-phase work even above currently more profitable Extract activity.
  • The Beginnings of an Idea: XP is Long Volatility Kent Beck from Software Design: Tidy First? · 2026-07-14 Reader 🖥️
    Beck's genuinely new ideas — including the just-arrived analogy 'XP is long volatility' — take years of unconscious incubation before they're ready to articulate, and once ripe they can only be sharpened into a teachable form by repeatedly explaining them badly, in varied ways, to real audiences, not by solitary polishing.
  • Itchy Brain (Still Burning: Michael Grinich & Kent Beck on AI adoption, enterprise developer tools) Kent Beck — Still Burning Podcast · 2026-05-20 Reader 🖥️
    We are not in another technology hype wave but in a step-function transformation: the marginal cost of producing software is collapsing toward zero, large incumbents are adopting AI faster than any prior shift, and the whole ecosystem — not just the AI labs — is accelerating; in this 'Red Queen' regime, profit and productivity may not visibly improve because every competitor is running at the new tempo, so the only sane response is to paddle straight into the wave by sustaining the maker's compulsion (the 'itchy brain'), reshaping engineering leadership toward hands-on impact, and accepting that software engineering as a profession will resemble medicine or law more than the unique 2010s template that minted six-figure undergrads.
  • Starving Genies Kent Beck · 2026-04-03 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    The simultaneous usage throttling by AI model providers is best understood not as an engineering constraint but as a narrative constraint: all three face the same investor-story shelf life, forcing synchronized demand-side rationing while the race to bend the supply curve up determines who wins the next wave.

  • incompleteThe framework gives no prospective way to diagnose which phase a project is currently in. Every exit criterion offered is retrospective — Explore's exit is 'one experiment works way better than others,' Expand's is 'cause and effect of growth become predictable,' Extract's is 'no further return on investment' — each recognizable only after the transition has already happened. Since Beck's highest-stakes claim is that applying the wrong-phase approach kills ideas, and phase identification is the one capability that claim depends on, the piece asserts the stakes of misdiagnosis while leaving diagnosis itself unaddressed.
    Canon 3X: Explore/Expand/Extract — read next: Read against lean-startup / product-discovery literature on leading indicators of product-market fit, which attempts exactly this real-time diagnosis problem; also reread Explore's own tactic ('quickly discard failures') for whether Explore's fast-failure discipline is itself an implicit workaround for the missing diagnostic — i.e., you don't diagnose the phase, you make Explore cheap enough that misdiagnosis is affordable.
