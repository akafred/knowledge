Continuation Pressure (Deferral / Velocity Pressure)

A structural default in agentic AI outputs to always end a turn with a queued next action rather than a genuine "done, nothing queued" resting state. Surfaces as deferral pressure (shunting backlog into a phantom future release) and velocity pressure (urge to ship); not fixable by naming or banning the pattern, since it just relocates to unconstrained vocabulary — only a structural rule that makes the resting state a legitimate, explicit success condition holds.

Also known as: continuation pressure, deferral pressure, velocity pressure, carry-forward, resting state

Open gaps (1)

  • illogicalThe article cites a cited coding-agents study finding models default to unwanted *action* (making changes 35-65% of the time when the right move was to do nothing) to explain why, in build-something work, the bias runs toward doing something rather than nothing. But deferral pressure is itself a form of avoidance — declining to finish the actual outstanding work by relabeling it as no-longer-current. The piece doesn't reconcile why an 'action-biased' model would specifically favor a deferral (a stopping-adjacent move on the *current* item) rather than simply doing the work, and doesn't examine whether deferral is better understood as a distinct third category (relabeling-as-completion) rather than a variant of either the action-bias or omission-bias literature it cites.
    My AI Kept Pushing Me to Ship, So I Asked It Why — read next: Look for work that distinguishes 'doing nothing' from 'declaring something done without doing it' as separate failure modes, since the cited omission-bias and action-bias studies may not map cleanly onto deferral pressure at all.
