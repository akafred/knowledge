RLHF / Post-Training Pipeline (SFT → Reward Model → RLHF)

The three-stage training pipeline (SFT on human demonstrations → reward model on preference comparisons → RL/PPO optimization against the reward model) that transforms a raw pretrained model into an instruction-following, safety-aligned assistant — originated in OpenAI's InstructGPT paper and still the conceptual foundation for post-training.

Also known as: RLHF, reinforcement learning from human feedback, supervised fine-tuning, SFT, reward model, reward modeling, preference data, preference model, PPO, proximal policy optimization, rollout, KL penalty, KL divergence penalty, policy model, InstructGPT pipeline, alignment tax

Documents (1)

  • The Post-training Process OpenAI Used for ChatGPT Sharon Zhou · 2026-09-21 Reader 🖥️
    The behaviors that make frontier models like ChatGPT feel conversational, safe, tool-capable, and intelligent are not emergent side effects of pretraining scale but engineered outcomes of the post-training pipeline — supervised fine-tuning on human demonstrations, reward modeling on human preference comparisons, and reinforcement learning (PPO) against that reward model — and each stage of that pipeline produces a measurable, stacking improvement in response quality over the last.

  • incompleteThe is-ought argument assumes AI cannot develop functional analogs to emotion or sympathy. It treats the Humean framework as requiring literal biological evolution, ignoring the possibility that sufficiently complex information processing systems might develop emergent motivational states or that sympathy could be architecturally encoded.
    AI Alignment Is Impossible — read next: Affective computing, computational models of empathy, functionalist philosophy of mind (e.g., can functional states play the role of emotions?). Also work on reward modeling as a form of value encoding.
  • incompleteGeneralization across model scale, architecture, and training recipe is largely untested in this summary post. Findings are drawn from specific Claude models (Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.6) plus one independent replication on an open-weights model via Neuronpedia (credited to Neel Nanda's commentary) — leaving open whether J-space-like structure is a general property of transformer LLMs at scale or partly an artifact of Anthropic's own post-training recipe (e.g. Constitutional AI / RLHF choices), especially given the post's own finding that post-training substantially reshapes what enters the J-space.
    A global workspace in language models — read next: Read Neel Nanda's commentary (linked in the piece) for the specifics and limits of the open-weights replication.
