Blue Ocean Strategy

Kim & Mauborgne's framework for finding uncontested market demand by diagnosing what excludes an industry's noncustomers, rather than competing on existing offerings within a crowded market.

Also known as: blue ocean, red ocean, noncustomers, value innovation, Kim and Mauborgne

Documents (2)

  • Welcome to AI Autumn Gretchen Gavett · 2026-09-08 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Two distinct claims coexist under one masthead: leaders can find profitable new market space not by outcompeting rivals but by removing industry-imposed pain points for excluded noncustomers (illustrated via Midea's air-conditioner redesign), and HBR's own newsletter readership currently ranks 'leading through change and uncertainty' as its top self-reported leadership challenge, ahead of workload, career advancement, influence, and organizational politics.
  • Introducing The Founder Mindset: Tim Ferriss on Experiments, Risk, and Freedom HBR IdeaCast (Adi Ignatius, bonus crossover episode featuring Reza Satchu interviewing Tim Ferriss) · 2026-07-30 Reader 🖥️
    Ferriss argues that what looks like reckless risk-taking is really a disciplined practice of minimizing downside — defining fears concretely instead of vaguely, treating decisions as either fast-and-reversible or slow-and-irreversible, and pursuing 'bounded' optionality (a pre-narrowed, time-boxed set of parallel experiments) rather than either indefinite hedging or premature commitment — which he claims reconciles his love of optionality with Satchu's thesis that commitment is a superpower.

Open gaps (2)

  • incompleteThe piece quotes only 2 of the 8 diagnostic questions Kim & Mauborgne propose, and never shows what Midea actually changed (price point, installation model, product design) or what business results followed (share, revenue, adoption) — the case study asserts an insight but not its execution or outcome.
    Welcome to AI Autumn — read next: The new/enhanced edition of Kim & Mauborgne's blue ocean strategy book itself, for the full eight-question framework and the Midea case in detail.
  • incompleteThe piece doesn't engage the standard critique of blue ocean strategy: that a genuinely uncontested market space rarely stays uncontested once demand and profitability are demonstrated (competitors imitate, the 'blue ocean' turns 'red'), and that framework case studies are drawn from known successes (NVIDIA, Midea) rather than from a representative sample of firms that tried the same diagnostic and failed — a survivorship-bias problem common to consulting-style strategy frameworks.
    Welcome to AI Autumn — read next: Critical strategy literature on first-mover advantage erosion and imitation dynamics, or empirical follow-up studies on blue ocean strategy's track record since its 2005 introduction.
