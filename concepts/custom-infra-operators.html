Custom Infrastructure Operators (Extending Platform Primitives)

Building bespoke operators/controllers that extend a platform's native primitives (Kubernetes PDBs, node-pool management) to solve problems the platform doesn't natively support — e.g. spanning an invariant across cluster boundaries, or collapsing per-node-pool config into a single higher-level abstraction.

Also known as: custom kubernetes operator, cluster lifecycle operator, clo, x-pdb, cross-cluster pod disruption budget, extending built-in primitives

Part of: Platform Engineering

Documents (4)

  • Google Open-Sources AX a Kubernetes Style Orchestrator for Autonomous AI Agents Olimpiu Pop · 2026-09-22 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Google's AX is a Kubernetes-style, open-source control plane that treats AI agents as stateful, checkpointable actors on a purpose-built runtime (Agent Substrate) rather than as microservices or batch jobs, solving the idle-compute and cold-start economics of long-running agentic workloads at the price of inheriting full Kubernetes operational complexity — a trade-off the technical community is already visibly split on rather than one the article itself adjudicates.
  • Presentation: How To Run on Three Clouds at Once, and When Not To Ross McFarlane, Kevin Holditch · 2026-09-11 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Form3 proves that a regulated payments platform can run truly active-active-active across three cloud providers by treating each cloud as nothing more than another availability zone and building custom Kubernetes operators to extend the platform's own primitives across cluster boundaries where they run out — but the same team's parallel US disaster-recovery build shows that this triple-active design is not a universal best practice: it is justified only when a specific market's resilience expectations, a mature platform-engineering practice, and a willingness to pay a real cost premium all line up, and even then the harder problem — deciding, mid-incident, whether to actually trigger a failover — remains an unresolved judgment call under uncertainty rather than something engineering can fully automate away.
  • Presentation: Fixing the AI Infra Scale Problem by Stuffing 1M Sandboxes in a Single Server Felipe Huici · 2026-09-09 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    The historical trade-off between isolation strength and speed/density in cloud infrastructure was never intrinsic to the hypervisor boundary itself — it was an engineering artifact distributed across the whole platform chain (proxy, controller, VMM, snapshot storage, host-OS resource limits) — and once each link is rebuilt for millisecond latency, snapshot-based stateful scale-to-zero turns the (still gold-standard) VM isolation boundary into the highest-density primitive available for bursty, mostly-idle agent and sandbox workloads.
  • Netflix Moves Toward Open Source Flink Autoscaler for 30,000+ Streaming Jobs Leela Kumili · 2026-09-07 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Reasoning about autoscaling at the level of individual dataflow operators (via true processing rate estimated from throughput and busy time) rather than the whole cluster is a strictly better model for scaling complex, heterogeneous, stateful streaming pipelines — good enough that Netflix, despite years of investment in its own cluster-level autoscaler, is migrating 30,000+ jobs to the open-source Apache Flink Autoscaler and building new internal control-plane infrastructure around it rather than continuing to extend its bespoke system.

But Kubernetes's own HA and self-healing guarantees — which the presenter explicitly cites as reasons people value Kubernetes — depend on accurate liveness/readiness signals; a control plane fed a false world-model cannot correctly diagnose a genuinely stuck or crashed VM versus one that is merely asleep, undermining exactly the properties (self-healing, HA) the talk claims to preserve.
