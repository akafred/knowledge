Pluralistic Ignorance & Adaptation vs. Endorsement

The mismatch between visible behavioral conformity (driven by power-weighted norms and individual adaptation to them) and the actual, much wider diversity of private belief underneath — people mistake others' adaptation for endorsement and systematically underestimate how many privately disagree.

Also known as: power-weighted norms, adaptation is not endorsement, preference falsification, visible conformity vs private belief, the weirdo effect

  • TBM 440: The Problem With Putting People in Boxes John Cutler · 2026-09-19 Reader 🖥️
    Putting people in boxes — typing them by personality, strengths, or 'culture fit' — is a category error at work because behavior is not a stable, context-free trait but a function of the person interacting with situational incentives, norms, power dynamics, and constraints, so the operative question should be 'under what conditions do you/they behave differently' rather than 'what type are you.'
  • TBM 438: Am I the Only Weirdo in This Company? John Cutler from The Beautiful Mess · 2026-09-05 Reader 🖥️
    Organizations look far more uniform in belief than they actually are because what you observe is a compound of three layered things — power-weighted norms, individual adaptation to those norms, and the actual diverse beliefs of the people underneath — and we systematically mistake the middle layer (visible adaptation) for the bottom layer (real belief) while underweighting the top layer (power) that is actually driving what gets displayed.
