Viable Systems Model (Management Cybernetics)

A recursive management-cybernetics framework (Stafford Beer, 1960s) holding that any viable system or subsystem, at any level, must answer the same structural questions (identity, coordination, fit, environment, purpose) to remain viable — applied to org design as an alternative to product/autonomous-team framing.

Also known as: vsm, stafford beer, management cybernetics, value center, five questions (rohrer)

  • Beyond Autonomous Teams in Software Product Development Ben Linders · 2026-09-10 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Because a team's contribution in a complex organization is really a share of an ever-shifting web of value dependencies rather than a discrete 'product,' organizations should replace 'autonomous product team' thinking with 'value center' thinking, in which every unit at every level (person, team, team-of-teams, org) answers the same five recursive questions and trades off autonomy against coherence according to the shape of the value it carries, not according to ideology.

Open gaps (3)

  • incompleteThe claim that the five questions apply unchanged 'at all levels: team, team-of-teams, department, whole organisation' is asserted but only ever demonstrated with a single team-level worked example (Scrum backlog, sprint goal); no worked example is given at the team-of-teams, department, or whole-organization level, so the universality/recursiveness claim is untested within the piece itself.
    Beyond Autonomous Teams in Software Product Development
  • incompleteThe cybernetic framing (feedforward/feedback, Ashby's Law) is adopted as the organizing metaphor but not critically examined. Control theory assumes a well-defined desired state and measurable error signal — software development often has neither.
    Harness engineering for coding agent users — read next: Read Stafford Beer's work on viable systems and cybernetic management to assess how well control-theory concepts transfer to inherently creative/ambiguous engineering tasks.
