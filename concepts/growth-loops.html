Growth Loops & Systemic Feedback

The systems-thinking view of growth as a tug-of-war between a self-reinforcing loop (accelerating growth) and an inhibiting/balancing loop (capping it) — the stocks-and-flows lens applied to how ideas, products, and organizations actually grow and stall.

Also known as: growth loop, growth loops, Growth loop (reinforcing loop), reinforcing loop, Inhibiting loop, inhibiting loop, balancing loop

Documents (2)

  • Reject Change, Sometimes Kent Beck · 2026-09-02 Reader 🖥️
    Because a growing product's payoff structure differs by 3X phase — roughly symmetric win/loss during Extract, sharply upside-skewed during Explore, and ruin-inclusive (win/die/switch) during Expand — the correct exposure to volatility differs sharply too: Extract calls for the disciplined, rebalancing 'Shannon's Demon' strategy of protecting revenue and keeping changes reversible even at some cost to upside (i.e., deliberately rejecting some change), Explore calls for going all in on volatility despite that same strategy's mathematical dominance in symmetric bets, and Expand calls for a third move — investing directly to reduce the probability of catastrophic failure — so 'embrace change' is not a universal software-development virtue but a phase-specific one.
  • Canon 3X: Explore/Expand/Extract Kent Beck · 2026-07-30 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Because a growing idea passes through three phases — Explore, Expand, Extract — that each demand categorically different, even incompatible approaches to finance, team size, strategy, personnel, and risk, applying one phase's playbook to a project actually in a different phase kills the idea; managing a portfolio well means correctly matching each project's approach to its actual phase, and prioritizing Expand-phase work even above currently more profitable Extract activity.
