Decision-Grade Analytics

An operating model that starts analytics from the decision it is meant to improve rather than the question a user wants answered — labeling questions as descriptive/predictive/causal, exposing assumptions and counterfactuals, governing metric definitions, and risk-triaging validation — the corrective to "self-service" analytics that just gives people faster answers.

Also known as: decision-grade, decision-service analytics, self-service analytics (as foil), labeling the question, descriptive vs predictive vs causal question, incrementality

  • Netflix Open-Sources Agentic Workflow for Causal Inference Anthony Alford · 2026-08-18 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    When ground-truth verification is unavailable, an agentic workflow for causal inference earns trust not through provable output correctness but through an actor-critic loop, documented failure-mode playbooks, and process transparency that keeps every artifact human-inspectable, and Netflix's open-sourced oci-agent operationalizes exactly this pattern.
  • Research: What Physical Retail Stores Deliver in a Digital Economy Ayşe Çetinel, Gürhan Kök, Robert Rooderkerk · 2026-08-18 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    A physical store's value should be measured and organized around its total, causally-isolated contribution to enterprise-wide customer value across channels and the whole customer journey — not by siloed channel P&L or raw post-opening sales — and that value is concentrated in specific customer-journey moments and product categories, so investment should be matched to where it demonstrably helps rather than spread evenly across the assortment.
  • Grab Cuts Mechanical Analytics Work From 44% to 30% with AI Agents Leela Kumili · 2026-08-17 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    Grab reduced the mechanical share of its analysts' work by deploying AI agents across a staged autonomy model, an agent-routing system, and continuously maintained certified data context, while deliberately keeping humans accountable for metric definitions, causal interpretation, and final decisions.
  • Don't Let AI Make Bad Analytics Worse Steven Tadelis · 2026-07-27 source ↗ Reader 🖥️
    AI will make analytics abundant but not automatically better, so companies must deliberately build a 'decision-grade' operating model — starting every analysis from the decision it should improve, labeling descriptive/predictive/causal questions correctly, exposing assumptions and counterfactuals, governing metric definitions, risk-triaging validation of high-stakes claims, and rewarding truth-seeking over persuasion — or AI will simply accelerate existing analytical dysfunction.
