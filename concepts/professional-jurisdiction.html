Professional Jurisdiction (Abbott)

Andrew Abbott's sociology-of-professions framework — professions hold jurisdiction over work by winning it across three arenas (legal rules, public opinion, daily practice); jurisdiction erodes when belief in a task's necessity fades, independent of whether workers are literally replaced.

Also known as: jurisdiction, system of professions, three arenas of jurisdiction, Abbott's jurisdiction framework, law / public opinion / daily work, claiming new ground, getting skipped (vs. replaced)

  • You won’t get replaced. You’ll get skipped. Ernie Svenson · 2026-07-21 Reader 🖥️
    The right question about AI and law isn't whether AI will replace lawyers but whether AI erodes lawyers' jurisdiction — their claim over work, contested in three arenas (licensing law, public belief, and daily practice) per sociologist Andrew Abbott's 1988 framework — and because daily practice is the arena moving fastest, a solo or small-firm lawyer's best move is to proactively claim new jurisdictional ground with AI rather than defend the old ground with rules alone.
