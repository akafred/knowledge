TBM 432: Bundling & Unbundling Capabilities (and AI)

John Cutler · email · 2026-07-26 Reader 🖥️

AI as AmplifierCoordination CostExpert GeneralistOrganizational LegibilitySociotechnical Architecture
A role is only a temporary bundle of capabilities that are themselves distributed across people, teams, tools, and institutions, and those capabilities are always moving -- specializing, diffusing, centralizing, integrating, embedding, rebundling, externalizing, or being eliminated or lost -- in overlapping loops rather than a single linear stage; AI does not change the underlying physics of these motions, only the magnitude, speed, and feasibility of each one, so the honest response to 'what does AI change' is a set of open, motion-by-motion questions rather than a single verdict.

practical · expository essay building a nine-part taxonomy of capability motions, illustrated with a career anecdote and a headline-driven present-day scan, then applying it to AI — Classic Cutler 'Sunday-morning diagramming and thought-provocation': opens with a personal-career bundling/unbundling story, generalizes it into a taxonomy of nine capability motions plus a 'loops not stages' visual, walks the taxonomy against current AI headlines section-by-section, then closes -- as in TBM 433 -- by handing the practical question back to the reader ('Use them as language... Key terms

  • Role as a temporary bundle
    A job title or role is not a stable unit of capability -- it is a provisional grouping of capabilities that happen to sit together right now; the capabilities themselves live independently across people, teams, routines, tools, platforms, and institutions.
  • Capability motions (Specialization, Diffusion, Centralization, Integration, Embedding, Rebundling, Externalization, Elimination, Loss)
    Nine distinct ways a capability can move through a system over time -- Cutler's granular alternative to talking about 'roles changing' or 'jobs disappearing.'
  • Loss (vs. Elimination)
    A specific, worse motion: work disappears before the underlying capability -- the judgment, context, relationships it sustained -- has been reproduced elsewhere. Distinguished sharply from Elimination, where work genuinely disappears without that cost.
  • Loops, not stages
    Capability motions do not proceed through a single linear sequence; many loops run simultaneously at different levels and speeds, each motion creating pressure and tension for other motions.
  • The physics of a pattern (vs. its variables)
    Cutler's self-directed diagnostic question -- 'Does AI change the physics of this pattern?' -- distinguishing whether AI alters the underlying causal structure of an organizational dynamic versus merely changing the magnitude of its variables so dramatically that it looks new.
  • 'We know more than we can tell' (Polanyi, via diffusion)
    Applied specifically to whether AI can diffuse expertise: codified knowledge transfers via AI, but tacit, contextual, embodied expertise may have a diffusion ceiling that is invisible from outside the expertise itself.

Arguments

The right question about AI's organizational effect is not 'what does AI change' as a single verdict, but a set of motion-specific, condition-specific empirical questions. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • A system can obey the same underlying causal structure ('physics') while its variables change so dramatically that the pattern appears entirely new.
  • There is already evidence AI is affecting specialization, diffusion, centralization, integration, embedding, externalization, convergence, and loss simultaneously.
  • The unresolved questions are to what extent, under what conditions, and with what second- and third-order effects each motion is affected -- not whether it is affected.
Implicit premises:
  • A single aggregate verdict ('AI makes organizations more/less specialized') would obscure motion-level heterogeneity that actually determines organizational outcomes.
  • Treating capability as decomposable into distinct motions is itself a valid and useful abstraction, not an oversimplification of a genuinely unified phenomenon.
Inferences:
  • from the physics/variables distinction and the observed simultaneity of multiple motions, infer that only a motion-by-motion, conditional analysis is epistemically honest
Confident, general claims about AI's long-term organizational effects are currently unwarranted. strength: high
Explicit premises:
  • Hype makes adoption a signal in itself, decoupled from value produced.
  • Research on any change touching individuals, teams, incentives, and markets simultaneously cannot license general claims from bounded task-level studies.
  • Common mental models (factory production, natural-language querying replaces framing expertise, codebase as pile of code) are flawed and produce systematically wrong AI-impact assessments.
  • AI is a stack of curves (technical, economic, social) unfolding at different speeds, so 'early' or 'late' framings are too simple.
Implicit premises:
  • These four limitations (hype, research, flawed models, time) are jointly sufficient to undermine confidence in current claims, even though none alone is unique to AI.
  • Epistemic humility here is itself actionable guidance, not merely a disclaimer -- it should change how leaders talk about AI's effects (motion-language, open questions) rather than paralyze action.
Inferences:
  • from four independent, converging limitations, infer that current AI-organizational claims should be held provisionally

Narratives

  • Cutler's own early product-management career, in which he personally performed research, data analysis, wireframing, requirements-writing, release coordination, support, and even some coding -- all loosely bundled under 'product management' -- before those capabilities separated into distinct disciplines (design, analytics, product marketing, program management, product operations) over time.
    → grounds the abstract 'role as temporary bundle' thesis in a single lived, concrete instance before generalizing it into the nine-motion taxonomy -- the essay's entire argument is an induction from this one biography.
  • The MIT Work of the Future task force (Autor, Mindell, and Reynolds, 2020) warning that cutting out middle tasks can destroy workers' skills, networks, and career pathways even when immediate output looks intact.
    → supplies external, citable authority for the Loss motion's central worry, preventing it from reading as pure speculation.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Why do AI-era headlines about roles seem to contradict each other (broadening, disappearing, specializing, and merging all at once)?
    Because multiple distinct capability motions are occurring simultaneously and headlines collapse them into single catchy narratives.
  • ⭕ Does AI change the fundamental patterns of organizational capability, or just the scale/speed of existing patterns?
    Cutler poses this as his own open diagnostic question and answers it only provisionally, motion by motion, without a final unified verdict.
  • ⭕ Can AI actually diffuse deep expertise to more people, or only its codified surface?
    Left open, gated on whether Polanyi's tacit-knowledge ceiling holds under AI -- explicitly stated as currently unknown.
  • ✅ What happens to the human role once its expertise is embedded into AI tools?
    It shifts toward direction, assessment, and oversight -- genuinely different skills from the embedded ones.
  • ⭕ What conditions determine whether a given capability motion produces useful adaptation versus harmful instability?
    Not addressed; each motion's tension is described as capable of producing either outcome or both, with no criteria given for which prevails when.

Gaps

  • incompleteCutler names that each capability motion 'may produce useful adaptation, harmful instability, or both' but never supplies criteria, examples, or even a hypothesis for what determines which outcome dominates in a given case -- team maturity? psychological safety? existing coordination debt? Without this, the taxonomy is a vocabulary for description but offers no diagnostic or prescriptive power: two organizations undergoing the identical motion (say, Embedding) get no guidance on why one thrives and one degrades.
    Read next: Contingency-theory organizational design literature (e.g., Galbraith on organization design fit) for criteria that predict when a given capability reallocation helps versus harms; Donella Meadows on leverage points for a framework distinguishing which interventions matter more.
    closed byTBM 431: The Denominator That Matters — TBM 432's open gap asks what determines whether a capability motion tips toward useful adaptation or harmful instability, listing 'psychological safety' among unexamined candidate factors. TBM 431 supplies Cutler's own explicit candidate answer at the level of AI adoption generally: the Social Contract (whether gains will be captured against the people who generated them) -- though this is Cutler's asserted hypothesis, not empirically adjudicated, so the gap is narrowed rather than fully closed.
  • incompleteThe essay's Loss motion and its MIT-task-force citation name the risk that judgment, context, and career pathways disappear before being reproduced elsewhere -- but Cutler offers no mechanism, checkpoint, or organizational design that would let a leader detect Loss is happening before it has already happened (unlike, say, a lagging financial metric). The essay's own Conclusion asks 'which routine activities are helping people build judgment' as a discussion-starter question, implicitly admitting no answer exists yet.
    Read next: This corpus's own junior-pipeline/apprenticeship-collapse gaps (e.g. extract-research-why-some-junior-employees-work-well-with-ai-and-others-dont, the Hanselman and Reed extracts) for parallel unresolved attempts at the same mechanism question; Cappelli on internal talent architecture for a stock-and-flow framing of capability loss.
  • uninformedCutler asserts that AI 'hasn't eliminated the social work of coordination, negotiation, and shared judgment' between specialists and generalists (Integration subsection), but doesn't engage the possibility -- raised elsewhere in this reader's corpus about the same author's fiefdom essay -- that AI's friction-busting on translation and handoff costs could, over a longer horizon, shift the political economy of coordination itself (who needs whom, who can bypass whom), not just its transactional cost. He treats the political layer as static while granting the cost layer is dynamic.
    Read next: extract-cutler-ai-wont-save-kingdoms#g3 poses exactly this dynamic-vs-static coordination-cost question from the fiefdom side; reading the two together tests whether Cutler's own corpus is internally consistent on this point.
  • incompleteThe essay treats 'capability' as if it moves largely independent of formal organizational structure (reporting lines, budget ownership, team boundaries) -- but Cutler's own other writing (on defining teams, on the reverse Conway maneuver) argues architecture and org structure are hard constraints on how fast capability can actually be reallocated. This piece never reconciles the two: can a capability really 'rebundle' into a role if the team/reporting/architecture boundaries around it haven't moved, or does rebundling silently stall at the structural layer?
    Read next: extract-tbm-423-defining-teams (same author) directly argues architecture is a hard constraint underestimated by reorg efforts -- read the two Cutler pieces together for the missing synthesis.
