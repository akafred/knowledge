Key terms

  • Shaping doc Documentation Drift & Living Documentation
    The pre-build document produced in the Shape Up method that frames a bigger project (problem, solution outline, boundaries); here published on Notion as the project's source of truth.
  • Drift (after kickoff)
    The published shaping doc becoming out of step with what the team decided and changed once work is underway.
  • Visual diff (highlighted v2)
    A private v2 Notion doc in which Claude marks every new or changed passage with highlighting, so a human can review AI restructuring quickly without reading the whole doc.
  • Human scannable
    A doc a person can skim and trust at a glance; the standard that heavy human editing of AI-drafted text is meant to meet.
  • Merge
    A spoken command telling Claude to fold the approved private v2 back into the original published Notion doc.
  • Breadboard
    A Shape Up low-fidelity interface-flow sketch (places, affordances, connections) used in shaping; here generated by custom tooling from transcripts and rough sketches and embedded as interactive HTML.

Arguments

A tiered AI-assisted capture workflow, with a human approval gate at each tier, can keep a published shaping doc current after kickoff.
Explicit premises:
  • Localized changes are catchable from the project's Slack channel by a bot
  • Bigger changes can be captured by an LLM reading the doc and the call recording and proposing a v2
  • Visual changes can be regenerated by custom tooling driven by transcripts and sketches
Implicit premises:
  • Most doc-relevant decisions are made in Slack or recorded calls, so the sources the AI watches are complete
  • The AI captures decisions faithfully, or the human gate reliably catches where it does not
  • The human has the attention to keep reviewing each proposed update
Inferences:
  • Decisions made outside Slack and recorded calls (hallway talk, DMs, unrecorded meetings) will still cause drift
  • As update volume grows, the human approval step becomes the new bottleneck
Highlighting every change makes a restructured AI draft reviewable without sacrificing human readability.
Explicit premises:
  • Claude restructures the doc and the new structure often is not what the author wants for human-readability
  • Claude can mark every new or changed passage with highlighting
  • The author can then give light direction and merge
Implicit premises:
  • The model's highlighting is complete and accurate: nothing changed silently, nothing highlighted that did not change
  • Reading highlighted passages is enough to catch errors; unhighlighted text can be trusted
  • Human review attention, not model capability, is the scarce resource
Inferences:
  • A deterministic diff (e.g., Notion page history) could verify the model's self-reported diff
  • Silent unhighlighted edits are the characteristic failure mode and the one this workflow cannot see

Narratives

  • The author's own evolution: writing shaping docs by hand, then having AI draft and heavily editing, then building a three-tier maintenance pipeline; and from rendering breadboards as images to generating interactive HTML embeds after hitting Notion's zoom limits.
    → Shows the practices as iterated responses to concrete pain (readability, drift, Notion image handling), supporting the claim that the design is practitioner-tested.
  • The post-review-session scenario: after a session where the team reworks something, Claude reviews the Zoom recording and proposes a v2 with everything decided.
    → Illustrates the tier-2 trigger and why a diff (not just a rewrite) is needed.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ How do you keep a published shaping doc from drifting from what the team actually decided after kickoff?
    Three tiers: a Slack-watching bot for small changes with approval, a Claude-proposed highlighted v2 merged on command for big changes, and custom tooling for breadboards.
  • ✅ How do you let AI restructure a document without making it unreadable or untrustworthy to humans?
    Make the AI highlight every change so the draft doubles as a visual diff; the human reviews the highlights and directs by dictation.
  • ⭕ How do you verify the AI's capture and its highlighted diff is complete?
    Not addressed; trust in the model and the human gate is assumed.
  • ⭕ What happens to the shaping doc as the build proceeds and code diverges from it?
    Not addressed; the post covers keeping the doc aligned with decisions, not with implementation.

Gaps

  • incompleteThe highlighted-v2 'visual diff' is generated by the same model that made the edits, so completeness is self-reported: nothing checks that no change slipped in unhighlighted or that every highlight is a real change. The failure mode that matters (silent edits or dropped decisions) is exactly the one the review cannot see; a deterministic diff against the published doc (e.g., page history) is never mentioned.
    Read next: Read on delegated editing corruption and verification of AI-produced diffs; compare with deterministic diff/merge tooling and the corpus's work on verifying AI output.
  • incompleteCapture is limited to what flows through the watched Slack channel and recorded calls; decisions made in DMs, hallways, unrecorded calls, or in the author's head still cause drift. There is also no stated criterion for deciding which change tier a given change belongs to, or how contradictory later decisions are reconciled with earlier text.
    Read next: Look for decision-record practices (ADRs, decision logs) and work on capturing implicit decisions from team communication.
  • incompleteThe evidence is one practitioner's report ('Works great') on a single client, in a setup with bespoke personal tooling. Nothing is said about multi-editor teams, who may approve or merge, approval fatigue when updates multiply, privacy of feeding recordings to models, or any measure of drift reduction.
