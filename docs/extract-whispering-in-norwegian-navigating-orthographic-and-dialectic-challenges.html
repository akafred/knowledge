Whispering in Norwegian: Navigating Orthographic and Dialectic Challenges

Per E Kummervold, Javier de la Rosa, Freddy Wetjen, Rolv-Arild Braaten, Per Erik Solberg · pdf · 2024-02-02 Reader 🖥️

Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)Data SovereigntyEvalsOpen-Weight Models
NB-Whisper — OpenAI's Whisper fine-tuned by the National Library of Norway on roughly 28,000 hours of curated, iteratively-cleaned Norwegian audio-text data from four sources (NRK subtitles, audiobooks, the NST corpus, and the Stortinget speech corpus) — substantially reduces word-error-rate on Norwegian Bokmål and Nynorsk transcription (e.g. 10.4→6.6 on Fleurs, 6.8→2.2 on NST, 30.0→12.6 on Common Voice Nynorsk) by explicitly targeting the dual written standards and pervasive dialectal/orthographic variation that generic Whisper handles poorly, though an inherent ~1-2% WER floor from legitimate Norwegian spelling variation and unresolved hallucination/streaming limitations mean the problem is substantially advanced rather than solved.

theoretical · expository — An empirical NLP systems paper: it reports what was built and measured (a fine-tuned ASR model and its word-error-rate results) rather than prescribing action. Structured like a standard ML conference paper (intro, related linguistic background, architecture, dataset, evaluation, results, limitations, future work) with argumentative passages defending specific design choices (why not teacher-student pseudo-labeling, why light normalization) embedded in an otherwise expository report.

Structure

PartRole
IntroductionMotivates the gap: Whisper is state-of-the-art but its weak-supervision approach, tuned mostly on English/high-resource data, underperforms on Norwegian's dialects and dual orthography.
Language Variability and ASR ChallengesEstablishes the linguistic facts an ASR system for Norwegian must handle: two co-official written standards (Bokmål, Nynorsk), intra-standard orthographic variation, and dialects with no official spoken norm, evidenced by e.g. 38 recorded pronunciations of 'hvem'.
Model Architecture and TrainingDescribes retaining Whisper's encoder-decoder Transformer architecture while modifying hyperparameters (batch size 1024, increased BPE dropout, activation dropout instead of stochastic depth) and running a two-phase training schedule (200k steps, then 50k steps on a cleaned subset).
DatasetDetails the four training sources (NRK subtitles, audiobooks, NST, Stortinget Speech Corpus; ~22,184h stage 1 / 6,078h stage 2), the alignment techniques (subtitle timestamps, NB-Wav2Vec2 forced alignment), English-translation augmentation, and a multi-criteria automated cleaning pipeline (fuzzy first/last-word matching, insertion/omission n-gram detection, NER-based filtering).
Experimental Setup and EvaluationDefines the three Norwegian test sets used (Fleurs, NST, Common Voice Nynorsk) and the WER methodology (JiWER, lowercased, punctuation-stripped, non-normalized for cross-dataset comparability).
Results and DiscussionReports WER across five model sizes (Tiny→Large) and three datasets, showing NB-Whisper beats OpenAI Whisper at every size, with the largest relative gains on Nynorsk and at smaller model sizes.
Challenges and LimitationsAcknowledges that hallucination and long-text transcription were only evaluated qualitatively (no suitable Norwegian benchmark exists), and that the autoregressive, 30-second-chunk architecture is a poor fit for real-time/live transcription.
Future WorkProposes using an LLM to harmonize orthographic variation in the training corpus to a consistent style, and re-cleaning the dataset with improved tooling.
ConclusionRestates the achievement and frames NB-Whisper's methodology as transferable to other dialect-rich, dual-standard languages.

Key terms

  • Word Error Rate (WER) Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)
    The paper's central metric, computed with the JiWER package after lowercasing and stripping punctuation, deliberately reported non-normalized so scores are directly comparable across the three test datasets; the authors note that a stronger normalization (e.g. spelling out numbers, as in a companion Wav2Vec2 paper) would have produced lower, but less comparable, scores.
  • Bokmål / Nynorsk
    Two co-official written standards that any Norwegian ASR output must consistently target regardless of the speaker's dialect — the paper treats 'correct transcription' as convergence on one of these written norms, not a phonetic rendering of speech.
  • Dialect
    Spoken variation that is decoupled from the written standard: there is no official spoken norm, dialects are used even in parliament and news broadcasts, and a single interrogative pronoun ('hvem'/'kven') has 38 recorded pronunciations in the Nordic Dialect Corpus — dialect is presented as an input-side nuisance variable the model must normalize away, not something to preserve in output.
  • Weak supervision (Whisper-style pretraining)
    Pretraining on a large, loosely-aligned corpus of subtitles (as opposed to a verbatim phoneme-to-grapheme dataset) that lets the model learn punctuation, capitalization, and natural written-language phrasing in one step — inherited from Whisper and extended here with Norwegian-specific weakly-aligned sources (NRK subtitles, Stortinget proceedings).
  • Dataset cleaning via own-model filtering
    A specific two-stage procedure: train on the full noisy dataset, run the resulting model's own predictions against the training targets, and use four criteria (fuzzy first/last-word match, detection of target insertions absent from any prediction, detection of omissions present in predictions but absent from target, NER-based filtering) to delete bad snippets — explicitly not a teacher-student pseudo-labeling approach, since the model's outputs are used only to filter, never as new training targets.
  • Orthographic variation
    The existence of multiple, simultaneously 'correct' spellings for the same word within a single written standard (e.g. Bokmål 'lua'/'luen', Nynorsk infinitives '-e'/'-a') — treated as an inherent ceiling on achievable WER, not a model deficiency: even professionally narrated, force-aligned audiobooks only reach ~1-2% WER because of this.

Arguments

NB-Whisper achieves substantially lower word-error-rate than OpenAI's stock Whisper for both Norwegian Bokmål and Nynorsk transcription. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • Fine-tuning was performed on ~28,000 hours of Norwegian-specific audio-text data from four curated sources.
  • Hyperparameters (batch size, BPE dropout, activation dropout, weight decay) were tuned specifically for this corpus.
  • A two-stage training regime with automated, multi-criteria data cleaning between stages was used to reduce hallucinations.
  • Evaluation was run on three independent Norwegian test sets (Fleurs, NST, Common Voice Nynorsk) using a fixed WER methodology (JiWER, lowercased, punctuation-stripped).
Implicit premises:
  • The chosen test sets (Fleurs, NST, Common Voice) are representative proxies for real-world Norwegian ASR use, including realistic dialect diversity and both written standards — despite Fleurs and NST both being read/scripted speech rather than spontaneous conversation.
  • WER computed on lowercased, punctuation-stripped text is a meaningful proxy for practical transcript usability, even though it discards capitalization and punctuation quality that Whisper-style models are specifically praised elsewhere in the paper for producing.
  • Confirming no speaker overlap between train and test splits is sufficient to rule out leakage-inflated scores; no other overlap check (e.g. content/topic overlap between NRK training data and NST/Fleurs) is claimed.
  • An improvement over OpenAI Whisper's numbers constitutes meaningful progress on 'the' Norwegian ASR problem, even though the paper itself documents an inherent ~1-2% orthographic-variation floor and unresolved hallucination/streaming limitations.

Norwegian's dual written standards plus pervasive, spoken-norm-free dialectal variation are the primary reason generic Whisper underperforms on Norwegian, and are what NB-Whisper is specifically engineered to address. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • The interrogative pronoun 'hvem'/'kven' has 38 distinct recorded pronunciations in the Nordic Dialect Corpus.
  • Bokmål and Nynorsk differ in inflectional forms, vocabulary, and some syntax, and each independently allows significant orthographic variation.
  • There is no official norm for spoken Norwegian, and dialects are used even in parliament and broadcast news.
  • Written transcripts (meeting notes, subtitles, parliamentary records) are expected to render speech consistently in one written standard regardless of the speaker's dialect.
Implicit premises:
  • Correct ASR output is defined as convergence on the institutionally-expected written standard, not a phonetically faithful rendering of the speaker's dialect — a normative choice about what 'correct transcription' means, presented as self-evident rather than argued.
  • Dialectal and orthographic variation are asserted to be the dominant driver of Norwegian ASR difficulty, ahead of other plausible factors such as domain mismatch, audio quality, or Norwegian's status as a lower-resource language for pretraining — this ordering is asserted, not isolated experimentally within the paper.

Narratives

  • The Bokmål feminine/masculine noun pair 'lua'/'luen' ('the hat') and the Nynorsk infinitive pair 'å elske'/'å elska' ('to love').
    → Illustrates orthographic variation within a single written standard — grounding the abstract claim that Norwegian permits multiple simultaneously correct spellings, which the paper later uses to explain the ~1-2% irreducible WER floor.
  • 'hvem' (Bokmål) / 'kven' (Nynorsk) for 'who', recorded with 38 distinct pronunciations in the Nordic Dialect Corpus.
    → Illustrates the scale of dialectal variation an ASR system must absorb before even reaching the written-standard mapping problem.
  • NB-Whisper Tiny scoring 28.0% WER on Common Voice Nynorsk versus OpenAI Whisper Tiny exceeding 100% WER on the same test.
    → A dramatic illustrative case showing that the fine-tuning payoff is largest exactly where the general-purpose model fails most completely — small models on the less-resourced written standard — reinforcing the paper's framing of Nynorsk as the harder, more neglected half of the problem.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Can OpenAI's Whisper be adapted to substantially improve Norwegian ASR accuracy across both official written standards?
    Yes — NB-Whisper roughly halves to two-thirds WER versus stock Whisper Large v3 across the Fleurs, NST, and Common Voice Nynorsk test sets.
  • ✅ What data sourcing and cleaning pipeline can turn noisy, loosely-aligned Norwegian audio sources (broadcast subtitles, parliamentary proceedings, audiobooks) into usable ASR training data?
    A four-source corpus (NRK subtitles, audiobooks, NST, Stortinget Speech Corpus) combined with a multi-criteria automated filter (fuzzy first/last-word matching, insertion/omission n-gram detection, NER-based filtering) applied between two training stages.
  • ⭕ Can NB-Whisper reliably handle hallucinations and long-text transcription?
    Only evaluated qualitatively; the authors state there are not enough detailed Norwegian datasets to test this quantitatively, and name it as future work.
  • ⭕ Is NB-Whisper suitable for live/real-time transcription?
    No — explicitly flagged as an architectural limitation (autoregressive decoder, 30-second chunking) requiring a different architecture for that use case.
  • ⭕ How does WER performance vary across specific dialects, rather than in aggregate per dataset/written-standard?
    Not addressed anywhere — dialect diversity is cited extensively as the motivating challenge, but no dialect-stratified evaluation is ever reported.

Gaps

  • incompleteDespite framing dialectal diversity as the central Norwegian ASR challenge (with the 38-pronunciation 'hvem' example), the paper never reports WER broken down by dialect group — only by dataset and written standard (Bokmål/Nynorsk). It is impossible to tell from this paper whether remaining errors concentrate on non-Oslo or minority dialects, which would be the direct empirical test of the paper's own motivating claim.
    Read next: Look for NB-Whisper follow-up work or model-card updates using the Nordic Dialect Corpus (or NST's dialect metadata) as a stratified evaluation set.
  • incompleteNo comparison against any Norwegian ASR system besides the authors' own prior NB-Wav2Vec2 1B model — no commercial cloud ASR (Google, Azure, Amazon) and no other multilingual open models (e.g. Meta's MMS or SeamlessM4T) are benchmarked, so NB-Whisper's standing relative to the broader Norwegian ASR market is unknown from this paper alone.
    Read next: Search for independent third-party benchmarks comparing NB-Whisper against commercial cloud ASR APIs for Norwegian.
  • incompleteNo ablation studies isolate which of the four data sources, or which of the four cleaning criteria (fuzzy matching, insertion detection, omission detection, NER filtering), actually drove the WER improvement. The recipe is reported in full but never decomposed, so a reader cannot tell which ingredient mattered most or whether some contributed little or even hurt.
    Read next: Check whether the NB-Whisper GitHub repo (nostram) or any successor NB AI Lab paper includes ablations over data sources or cleaning filters.
  • illogicalThe 'clear trend' language used to describe model-size scaling (Section 6) treats single-run WER point estimates — with no reported variance, confidence intervals, or repeated-seed training — as sufficient evidence of a definitive, generalizable relationship between model size and accuracy for Norwegian specifically.
    Read next: Compare against papers that report WER variance across training seeds or use statistical significance testing (e.g. bootstrap resampling over test-set WER) for ASR scaling claims.
  • incompleteThe real-time/streaming transcription limitation is named but not engaged with relative to existing community workarounds (e.g. sliding-window/VAD-based chunked streaming used by tools like whisper.cpp or faster-whisper) that already mitigate this for the base Whisper architecture the paper builds on.
    Read next: Look at whisper-streaming / faster-whisper VAD-chunking implementations to see how the wider Whisper ecosystem addresses real-time use without an architecture change.
