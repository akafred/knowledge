Advanced Context Engineering for Coding Agents (ace-fca.md)

Dex Horthy (HumanLayer) · article · 2026-10-08 source ↗ Reader 🖥️

Agentic LoopAgile & XPContext EngineeringContext Rot & Context LimitsMulti-Agent OrchestrationOrganizational Legibility
Solving hard problems in brownfield codebases requires designing your entire workflow — not just individual prompts — around keeping the context window in a 40-60% utilization sweet spot via a research/plan/implement cycle of frequent, intentional compaction, with human review concentrated at the research and plan layers because errors there compound into far more downstream bad code than a single bad line; and the open problem now is scaling this disciplined, human-engaged practice collaboratively across whole teams, not just a single highly motivated practitioner.

practical · narrative — A first-person practitioner essay structured as a journey (naive chatbot use -> ad-hoc restarts -> intentional compaction -> frequent intentional compaction) with embedded case studies, building toward a product pitch (CodeLayer) for scaling the practice across teams.

Key terms

  • frequent intentional compaction
    Designing the entire workflow — not just individual prompts — around context management, keeping context utilization in the 40-60% range throughout, via a repeated research/plan/implement cycle rather than one large push or occasional ad-hoc restarts.
  • context window quality dimensions
    Four things to optimize a context window for — correctness, completeness, size, and trajectory — with three ranked failure modes in order of severity: incorrect information (worst), missing information, and too much noise (least bad).
  • mental alignment
    (citing Blake Smith) The primary purpose of code review: keeping team members synchronized on how and why the codebase is changing, not primarily correctness-checking.

Arguments

Teams should adopt frequent intentional compaction — redesigning their whole workflow around a 40-60% context-utilization band via research/plan/implement — rather than ad-hoc restarts or one-off intentional compaction.
Explicit premises:
  • Context window contents are the only lever on output quality once the model is fixed
  • Occasional/ad-hoc compaction still lets utilization drift into the degraded 'dumb zone' between resets
  • A continuous, workflow-wide discipline keeps utilization consistently in the productive band rather than only after things have already gone wrong
Implicit premises:
  • The marginal cost of maintaining this discipline continuously (more structure, more artifacts, more token spend) is justified by the quality gain for the kind of complex, brownfield tasks the essay is scoped to — a tradeoff the essay doesn't quantify for simpler tasks
Inferences:
  • If the discipline's main cost is process overhead rather than model capability, then the open problem genuinely is organizational (team adoption, tooling, culture) rather than technical — which is exactly the problem the author says the company is now building a product around

Narratives

  • On a 300k-LOC Rust codebase (BAML) the author had never worked in, a first research pass was thrown out as wrong; a redone research pass, paired with a plan built on it, beat a parallel no-research plan on the same bug — the research-grounded PR was approved by the codebase's own maintainer without him knowing it was a demo.
    → Demonstrates, as a controlled-ish comparison (same bug, two competing plans), that the research step specifically — not just planning in general — is what produces a fix that fits existing codebase conventions rather than merely 'working.'
  • A second, unresolved failure: two team-weeks spent on an MCP streaming-HTTP keepalive race condition that spiraled into dead ends, named as evidence the workflow does not work for every problem.
    → Prevents the essay's case studies from reading as uniformly successful; explicitly offered to pre-empt the 'hyped sales guy' framing.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ What exactly should a context window be optimized for, beyond just 'staying under some size limit'?
    Four dimensions (correctness, completeness, size, trajectory), with incorrect information identified as strictly worse than missing information, which is worse than noise.
  • ⭕ Does this research/plan/implement discipline transfer to team members other than its inventor?
    Partially addressed with internal evidence (an intern's fast ramp-up), but not with independent, outside-the-company adoption data.

Gaps

  • incompleteThe intern-ramp-up and team-cost figures are offered as generalization evidence but remain internal to the author's own small company and network — the same limitation the corpus has already flagged for the companion talk (personal anecdotes from a founder's own team, no independent outcome measurement). A genuinely independent test of whether this transfers to a less-motivated or larger, less cohesive team at org scale is still missing.
    Read next: Look for independent case studies or post-mortems of research/plan/implement-style workflows adopted by teams with no connection to HumanLayer, ideally including teams that abandoned the practice, to test generalization beyond a founder-led, highly bought-in team.
  • incompleteThe Ralph Wiggum while-loop is presented as a contrasting technique the author takes seriously, but the essay never states why he chose frequent intentional compaction over it for his own team — e.g. whether it's about task type (brownfield vs. greenfield), risk tolerance, or team review bandwidth. The two approaches sit side by side without a stated decision rule for choosing between them.
    Read next: Read extract-voss-what-the-hell-is-a-loop and extract-11-tips-ai-coding-with-ralph-wiggum for the task-loop (Ralph) discipline-progression model, and compare its stated preconditions (small, well-scoped, externally-verifiable tasks) against this essay's research/plan/implement preconditions (complex, brownfield, requiring human judgment) to build the missing decision rule.
