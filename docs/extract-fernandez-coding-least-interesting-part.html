What happens when the coding becomes the least interesting part of the work

Obie Fernandez · article · 2025-12-12 source ↗ Reader 🖥️

Agent SecurityCoordination CostDomain Expertise as AI Force-MultiplierPair & Mob ProgrammingTaste & Judgment
Senior engineering value has always lived in mostly-tacit, pre-code judgment (pattern-recognition, abstraction level, blast radius, reversibility) rather than typing, which is why coding agents cannot yet substitute for it — but once senior engineers and their employers realize this, the organizational result will be a drastic contraction toward small teams of highly accountable, AI-empowered seniors who absorb roles that used to belong to juniors, PMs, business analysts, and designers.

Key terms

  • Senior thinking
    The largely tacit, fast, quiet judgment a senior engineer brings to a task — about shape, direction, blast radius, reversibility, social cost — which is almost never articulated, documented, or standardized by teams.
  • Blast radius Agent Security
    The scope of consequence if a change turns out to be wrong — how far it would spread and how hard it would be to contain or undo.
  • Pairing with an agent
    Working with a coding agent (e.g., Claude Code) in the same disciplined, conversational mode as pairing with a human — explaining not just what to build but how you're thinking about the problem, which forces tacit reasoning into the open.

Arguments

Senior engineering judgment is not currently replaceable by coding agents, and the organizational consequence of that fact is a drastic headcount contraction toward small teams of highly accountable seniors, not business as usual.
Explicit premises:
  • Most of what a senior engineer does is pre-code pattern recognition framed around shape, blast radius, and reversibility rather than syntax
  • This tacit knowledge is barely represented in frontier model training data because it is almost never written down or recorded
  • Coding agents execute the mechanical/typing part of the job extremely well, which was already the less valuable part for senior practitioners
  • Once senior engineers and their employers recognize this, the economically rational move is to concentrate work in a few highly accountable, AI-empowered seniors and remove the roles whose value depended on the old division of labor
Implicit premises:
  • Senior judgment can be exercised just as well by a 4-5-person senior team using AI as it could by the original 20-person team's division of labor, with no quality or coverage loss from the roles removed
  • The supply of senior engineers carrying this tacit judgment is effectively adequate going forward and does not need active replenishment from a junior/mid pipeline
Inferences:
  • If true at scale, this predicts a structural hollowing-out of the junior/mid tier well before any agent itself acquires 'senior thinking' — which would eventually choke off the supply of future seniors, since that judgment is explicitly described as learned over 'years and decades' doing exactly the work being eliminated. The author states the mechanism that implies this but never draws the consequence.

Narratives

  • When pairing with someone less experienced, the author slows down enough to explain parts of his 'senior thinking' but deliberately not all of it, because fully articulating it 'would bog us down too much.'
    → Shows concretely that senior tacit knowledge stays underdocumented even in the most favorable transmission context available — live pairing with a junior — setting up why pairing with an agent (which has no relationship to protect and no patience to spare) forces out loud what pairing with a junior colleague does not.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Can coding agents replace the judgment-heavy part of senior engineering work?
    Not currently, and not soon — that judgment is tacit, poorly represented in training data, and agents have no intrinsic determination to apply it even when they recognize its elements; the author doesn't expect this to change without something like real AGI.
  • ✅ Why do some programmers not see the point of coding agents, or actively dislike them?
    Because the part of the job they personally value is the physical act of writing code — exactly the part agents are best at replacing — so agents feel irrelevant or threatening rather than liberating, depending on what the engineer valued in the first place.
  • ⭕ What happens to the junior/mid engineers, project managers, business analysts, and designers whose roles depended on the old division of labor once seniors can direct AI directly?

Gaps

  • incompleteThe piece predicts collapsing a 20-person team into 4-5 AI-empowered seniors who absorb the PM/BA/designer roles, but its own argument undercuts this: senior judgment is explicitly described as learned 'over the course of years and decades' by doing exactly the junior/mid work the piece says will disappear first ('even the most productive juniors and mids are going to start feeling like net negatives'). If that pipeline closes, the piece never explains where the next generation of seniors — the only people it trusts to hold this irreplaceable tacit knowledge — will come from.
    Read next: extract-hbr-mentoring-matters-ai-era, which names this exact tension (the on-ramp for professional judgment disappearing alongside the flattening of the senior population expected to do the mentoring) as a structural risk to the leadership pipeline.
  • incompleteThe claim that cutting a team to 4-5 seniors removes the need for a project manager, business analysts, or designers treats those roles as pure overhead that disappears once seniors can ship directly, without engaging what those roles actually absorbed besides shippable output — stakeholder alignment, prioritization trade-offs, user research, cross-team coordination. That work doesn't vanish just because seniors can type code themselves; it has to land somewhere.
    Read next: extract-kill-your-org-chart-for-ai (a related claim that at least names 'the development of better judgment' as management's remaining purpose) and extract-ai-native-engineering-org (bottleneck shifts to alignment/decision-making, not away from coordination entirely).
  • uninformedThe claim that typing code 'feels like a waste of time' when billed at an expensive hourly rate treats the economics purely from the senior consultant's individual vantage; it doesn't address the same calculus for salaried engineers inside a company absorbing the AI spend, or whether clients will keep paying the same hourly rate once they also realize how much of the work is agent-executed.
