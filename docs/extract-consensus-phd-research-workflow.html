Effective AI-assisted literature research, as Alvina Lai practices it with Consensus, is not a single search step but a repeatable staged workflow — natural-language topic search over a peer-reviewed corpus, triage of algorithmically-flagged key papers, source capture into a library, verification of drafted claims against extracted evidence, a second targeted search to establish a literature gap, and (newly) citation-graph-based discovery — that replaces exact-keyword, paper-by-paper verification with faster, terminology-agnostic retrieval and writing-stage claim-checking.

practical · interview / product-demo testimonial, hosted and produced by the tool vendor's own channel — This is a scoped slice of a longer interview; only the research-workflow/tooling portion (roughly the back half of the video) is extracted, per the user's explicit request. The substantive psychology content about parasocial relationships and polarization is deliberately excluded.

Key terms

  • Deep Search
    A Consensus search mode she used to start her grant-proposal literature review from a plain-language description of her topic; returned 352 eligible papers with a stated search methodology and quality ranking, rather than requiring exact keywords.
  • Claims & Evidence
    A Consensus panel she consults while writing to check that the claims stated in her manuscript/proposal are backed by the strongest available evidence in the retrieved literature.
  • Citation Graph
    A newly released (≈1 week before this recording) visualization: from a 'seed' paper it surfaces (1) direct citations, (2) papers consistently co-cited alongside it, and (3) papers sharing similar bibliography; additional seeds can be added and the graph regenerated around the growing set. Note: this mechanism was explained on camera by the Consensus interviewer (Daniel), not by Alvina, since she had just said she'd never used it; Alvina herself, after seeing it demoed, spontaneously analogized it to 'Obsidian... but for academia.'
  • Library (Zotero integration)
    Consensus's built-in reference-saving feature; lets her upload/save sources directly inside Consensus instead of using the standalone Zotero browser extension on every PDF.
  • Ask the paper
    A chat-with-document feature; she uploads a paper (including her own drafts) and asks Consensus to explain or summarize it for fast comprehension when she doesn't have time for a full close read.
  • Key papers
    Consensus's algorithmically flagged subset of the most relevant/highest-quality results within a search; she treats these as the papers to read first and as primary citation sources.

Arguments

Consensus substantially reduced the time and effort needed to complete a literature-review-heavy grant proposal compared to her prior Google Scholar-based workflow. strength: low
Explicit premises:
  • Google Scholar required exact-keyword, one-paper-at-a-time claim verification and took about a month for a comparable master's proposal.
  • Consensus lets her search by natural-language topic description, surfaces quality-ranked 'key papers,' and provides Claims & Evidence for direct verification of drafted claims.
  • This PhD grant proposal took about two weeks with Consensus, under a time crunch.
Implicit premises:
  • The two proposals (master's vs. PhD) are similar enough in literature-review difficulty for the one-month vs. two-week comparison to isolate the tool's effect rather than her own growing research skill or topic familiarity.
  • The volume/difficulty of available literature didn't independently differ enough between the two topics to explain the speed difference on its own.
Inferences:
  • Faster, keyword-independent retrieval plus built-in evidence verification → less time spent sourcing and cross-checking claims → shorter overall proposal-writing time.
AI-assisted, peer-review-filtered search tools like Consensus solve the interdisciplinary terminology/equivocation problem that keyword-based literature search cannot. strength: low
Explicit premises:
  • Different fields use different terms for the same construct (e.g., 'parasociality' in her field vs. 'trust' in political science).
  • Google Scholar's exact-keyword search can't bridge that terminology gap.
  • Consensus's natural-language Deep Search retrieved a broad, quality-ranked set of 352 eligible papers from a single topic-description prompt.
Implicit premises:
  • Consensus's underlying retrieval performs genuine semantic/conceptual matching across the terminology gap, rather than simple synonym expansion of the literal prompt text.
Inferences:
  • A large, high-quality result set returned from one natural-language prompt → therefore Consensus solves cross-field terminology mismatch.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ How can a researcher search literature across disciplines that use different terminology for the same concept?
    Use natural-language topic prompts via Consensus's Deep Search instead of exact-keyword search — though the mechanism by which this bridges terminology gaps is not explained or demonstrated.
  • ✅ How does she identify a genuine literature gap to justify a grant proposal's novelty?
    Run a second, targeted search on existing/adjacent interventions, check whether they work long-term, and combine self-found papers with papers supplied by her PI.
  • ✅ How does she verify that her own written claims are actually supported by the literature?
    Claims & Evidence panel review during the writing stage, replacing her old sentence-by-sentence Google Scholar verification.
  • ✅ How does she organize and retain sources across a project?
    Consensus's new Zotero-integrated library replaces a manual per-PDF Zotero-extension workflow.
  • ⭕ Does Citation Graph actually improve literature discovery for her research?
    Left genuinely open — she flags it herself as untested ('I've no idea what it does yet') immediately before demoing it live for the first time.
