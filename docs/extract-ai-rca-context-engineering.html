AI Root Cause Analysis Shifts from Model Reasoning to Context Engineering

Mark Silvester · rss · 2026-07-25 Reader 🖥️

Agent HarnessAgentic LoopAgentic vs. Deterministic System DesignContext EngineeringEvalsFrontier Model Convergence / CommoditizationOpen-Weight ModelsSRE & Incident Response
The hard part of AI-assisted root cause analysis has shifted from model reasoning -- which recent evidence suggests is largely solved by frontier and even many open-weight models given the same prepared context -- to context engineering: the harness work of correlating signals and assembling a compact, unmisleading context before the model is ever called.

practical · mixed — Primarily an expository news report on Coroot's Nikolay Sivko's research, but the article is structured argumentatively around a single claim (harness > model for AI RCA) and closes with an explicit practical recommendation ('effort spent preparing context may pay off more than reaching for a larger model'), so it functions as a practical text wearing expository clothing.

Key terms

  • context engineering Context Engineering
    The discipline of curating the smallest set of high-signal, compact context supplied to a model before it reasons -- treated as the emerging hard problem in AI RCA and, per the article's closing citations (Anthropic, LangChain, Mezmo), in AI-agent engineering generally.
  • the harness
    The pipeline/logic that decides what data reaches the model and in what shape -- explicitly separated from the model's own reasoning as the second of RCA's 'two jobs' (Sivko's framing, endorsed by the author).
  • deterministic (RCA) design Agentic vs. Deterministic System Design
    An architecture that correlates signals into findings up front and hands the model one fixed, prepared context with no agent loop -- as instantiated by Coroot and (per the article) Dynatrace's Davis AI.
  • agent-based (RCA) design Agentic vs. Deterministic System Design
    An architecture that hands the model tools and lets it decide what telemetry to fetch as it reasons, i.e. an open-ended investigation loop.

Arguments

The harness -- the pipeline that assembles and correlates context before the model is called -- rather than the model itself, is now the binding constraint on AI RCA quality, so engineering effort should shift there. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • Reasoning was uniformly strong across most frontier and larger open-weight models when given the same well-prepared, single context.
  • A deterministic pipeline design lets you isolate whether a wrong answer came from the model or from the harness, unlike an agent-based design.
  • The correlation/preparation work, done deterministically, is what filters out (or fails to filter out) misleading signals before the model ever sees them.
Implicit premises:
  • The single Coroot benchmark scenario (one Chaos Mesh network-delay fault, with confounds the harness was built to anticipate) is representative of AI RCA difficulty in production generally.
  • Passing one designed scenario, with confounds the experimenter already knew about and deliberately included, is evidence that reasoning 'is basically solved' rather than evidence that this specific, anticipated confound was solved.
Inferences:
  • If harness quality dominates outcomes, then organizations facing AI RCA quality problems should audit and invest in their correlation/context pipeline before assuming they need a more capable (or more expensive) model -- the article draws this inference explicitly as its 'practical takeaway'.
  • The same reframing (separate the harness variable from the model variable to make failures diagnosable) likely generalizes beyond RCA to any AI-agent task where deterministic pre-processing is feasible.

Narratives

  • Coroot engineer Nikolay Sivko built one controlled scenario to separate 'can the model reason?' from 'does the harness feed it the right evidence?': a Chaos Mesh NetworkChaos experiment injecting delay between a catalogue service and its Postgres database, slowing queries and pushing the front-end into 502 errors, with the context deliberately including misleading signals (query timings inflated by network round-trip time). He ran the same ~9,800-token prompt -- asking for root cause, the cause-and-effect chain, and the immediate fix -- against eleven different models. Closed frontier models and most larger open-weight models passed (correctly naming the injected Chaos Mesh experiment and flagging the need to delete it and its schedule); lower down, Gemma 4 31B was the only self-hostable model to identify the root cause, while the larger Qwen3.6 35B and Qwen3 Coder Next both missed it.
    → This is the evidentiary backbone for the entire article -- it operationalizes the abstract claim 'reasoning is no longer the bottleneck' as a concrete, falsifiable test, and its pass/fail pattern across eleven models is what licenses the article's unity claim (propositions p1, p3, p4).

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Is AI capable of doing root cause analysis? ("Can AI do RCA?")
    Reframed rather than answered directly: Sivko argues this is 'the wrong question' because it conflates two separately measurable jobs (model reasoning vs. harness/context preparation); once separated, the article concludes model reasoning is largely adequate and the harness is the real open problem.
  • ⭕ Which architecture -- agent-based (tool-fetching) or deterministic (pre-correlated, single-context) -- is better for AI RCA?
    The article presents a genuine trade-off (agent-based flexibility for novel incidents vs. deterministic repeatability, debuggability and cost) without declaring an overall winner, though it notes an industry drift toward deterministic designs and ends by treating harness/context work as the shared, higher-leverage lever regardless of architecture.
  • ⭕ Whether a hybrid architecture -- deterministic by default, with an agent-based fallback for incidents outside the predefined correlation set -- would combine the reliability of one camp with the coverage of the other.

Gaps

  • incompleteThe 'reasoning is basically solved' verdict rests on a single experimental scenario (one Chaos Mesh network-delay fault type, with one set of misleading signals the harness designer already anticipated). There is no report of repeated trials of the same scenario (to check consistency), no second incident type, and no test of an incident whose confounds were not anticipated at harness-design time -- exactly the case where the article itself says agent-based designs would have an edge.
    Read next: Look for a multi-scenario or multi-trial replication of Coroot's benchmark, or for evaluation frameworks purpose-built to separate model-reasoning-quality from harness-quality across a broader set of incident types (e.g. AIOps/observability benchmark suites).
  • incompleteNo false-positive or confidence-calibration analysis is given. The experiment reports pass/fail on naming the correct root cause, but not what any model said when it was wrong (confidently wrong vs. uncertain), nor how often a model would confidently misdiagnose when the deterministic harness itself missed or mis-weighted a signal it wasn't built to anticipate -- the precise failure mode the article's own 'novel incidents' caveat implies is possible.
    Read next: Look for incident-response AI evaluations that report calibration/false-positive rates, not just accuracy, especially under harness-blind-spot conditions.
  • incompleteThe article reports, without comment, an inverse-scaling result at the sub-frontier tier: Gemma 4 31B -- the smallest self-hostable model named -- was the only one of the three to identify the root cause, while the larger Qwen3.6 35B and Qwen3 Coder Next both missed it on the identical, well-prepared context. Sivko's 'basically solved' verdict reads as scoped to the frontier tier, and the piece does report this split openly rather than hiding it, but it never asks why a smaller model outperformed larger peers given the same harness -- training data, instruction-following style, or some other model-level factor the harness explanation can't account for.
    Read next: Look for follow-up analysis of why Gemma 4 31B outperformed larger open-weight peers on this specific task, and for other reported cases of inverse scaling on well-prepared-context reasoning tasks.
