Key terms

  • harness (10% / 90% split) Agent Harness
    The model is only about 10% of what determines a coding agent's output; the remaining 90% is the harness — instructions, tools, guardrails, orchestration, observability — that the team, not the model provider, controls and can improve.
  • system evolution mindset
    When a coding agent produces a bad outcome that required manual correction, the response should not be to just fix that one output but to ask what rule, workflow, or part of the harness should change so the same class of failure is less likely next time — treating the harness itself as something that evolves, the same way a codebase does.
  • conductor / orchestrator
    Two modes a developer moves through: conductor = hands-on, single-file, keystroke-level steering (how AI coding assistance looked when tab-complete was new); orchestrator = reviewing outcomes of coding agents handling much larger, even multi-codebase, tasks. The author explicitly disagrees with Google's claim that developers should keep moving fluidly between the two — he argues that once the harness is reliable enough, a developer 'graduates' to living in orchestrator mode, reserving conductor-level granularity for deep debugging or initial exploration rather than as a recurring need.

Arguments

Developers should default to staying in 'orchestrator' mode once their harness is reliable, rather than treating frequent movement between conductor and orchestrator modes as a permanent operating pattern. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • Conductor-level, file-by-file steering was how AI coding assistance looked early on (tab-complete), before harnesses matured.
  • A reliable, well-engineered harness lets an orchestrator-level developer trust the agent's output without watching individual keystrokes.
  • Deep debugging and initial exploration are still times a developer may need to get granular.
Implicit premises:
  • Harness reliability is something a developer can accurately self-assess, rather than a false confidence that later produces unreviewed, lower-quality output.
  • The source material's claim (fluid movement between modes) was written for teams at an earlier, less mature stage of harness development, rather than being a durable claim about the work itself.
Inferences:
  • Teams that measure and publish their own harness reliability (e.g. via the same kind of benchmark testing Google cites) would have objective grounds for deciding when to graduate to orchestrator-only operation, rather than relying on a developer's subjective comfort.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ What actually determines whether an AI coding agent's output is good — the model or something else?
    The harness (90%), not the model (10%).
  • ⭕ Should a developer keep moving between hands-on and delegated modes of directing AI coding agents, or settle into one?
    The author disagrees with the source's 'keep moving between both' framing but offers no concrete criterion for when a team has earned the right to settle into orchestrator mode.
