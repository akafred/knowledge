Good system prompts, skills, and rules are not sufficient to make AI-generated code reliable because AI does not follow instructions reliably enough on their own; once prompt tuning hits diminishing returns, the more effective lever is strengthening feedback loops and guardrails -- strict lint rules, minimum test coverage, and automated review of AI-written code.

practical · argumentative — A short LinkedIn post making a single practical recommendation: when prompt engineering stops improving AI code quality, shift investment to automated feedback loops and guardrails rather than continuing to tune prompts.

Arguments

When AI code quality stalls despite prompt investment, shift effort to feedback loops and guardrails rather than continuing to refine prompts.
Explicit premises:
  • System prompts, skills, and rules do not reliably make AI follow instructions.
  • Adding feedback loops (lint, test coverage, auto-review) on top of prompts produces materially better results.
Implicit premises:
  • Feedback loops and prompt engineering are largely independent levers, so investment can be reallocated from one to the other without a corresponding tradeoff in the other direction.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Why do good system prompts fail to reliably produce quality AI-generated code?
    AI does not follow instructions (even with skills/rules layered on) reliably enough on its own.
  • ✅ What should a team do once prompt engineering stops yielding better results?
    Shift focus to strengthening feedback loops and guardrails: lint rules, minimum test coverage, automated review.
