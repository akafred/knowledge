Managers Are Not Overhead: They Are Infrastructure

David Michelson · article · 2026-07-22 source ↗ Reader 🖥️

AI as AmplifierAI Labor DisruptionCoordination CostEnterprise AI Adoption & ReadinessHierarchy & Org ChartsHuman as Orchestrator of AgentsOrganizational LegibilityPsychological Safety
Organizations flattening management layers on the assumption that AI has absorbed most of what managers used to do are making an unsupported productivity bet, because converging survey evidence shows managers have become the critical infrastructure layer that translates AI investment into realized team-level value, not a layer of overhead AI is now positioned to eliminate.

practical · argumentative — A short opinion essay structured as a rebuttal: it states an opposing narrative (flatten because AI absorbs manager work), then marshals two survey sources (Microsoft's 2026 Work Trend Index, LeadDev's Engineering Leadership Report 2026) against it, then draws a conceptual distinction (flattening vs. contraction) to reframe the diagnostic question for the next round of cuts.

Key terms

  • overhead
    a framing of managers as reducible administrative cost tied specifically to tasks (scheduling, reviews, coordination) that AI can now perform — the target framing the essay argues against
  • infrastructure
    the load-bearing organizational layer that converts an investment (in AI) into realized value at the team level — necessary substrate rather than optional layer
  • flattening Organizational Legibility
    removing management layers / widening span of control — explicitly distinguished from contraction; flattening can coexist with an expanding, not shrinking, managerial role
  • contraction
    narrowly defined as the scope of the managerial role itself shrinking because AI and ICs absorb more of the work — a specific claim the author argues the data does not support, as opposed to flattening
  • Transformation Paradox Enterprise AI Adoption & Readiness
    borrowed from Microsoft: the condition where employees move faster with AI than the organization around them adapts; managers are cast as the layer that resolves it

Arguments

Organizations cutting managers because AI has absorbed much of their traditional workload are making an unsupported productivity bet; managers should instead be understood and preserved as the infrastructure layer that lets AI investment convert into realized value. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • Manager behavior (modeling AI use, creating psychological safety) is strongly associated with employees' AI value, trust, and readiness (Microsoft Work Trend Index)
  • This effect is largest among the most advanced AI users, the population organizations most want to accelerate
  • If the managerial role were genuinely contracting under AI, early signs should already be visible; instead LeadDev's 2026 survey shows managers' scope, communication load, team count, and direct reports have all increased
Implicit premises:
  • The correlations reported by Microsoft (manager AI-modeling behavior <-> employee AI outcomes) reflect managers causing the improved outcomes, rather than reverse causation or a shared confound (e.g., already high-functioning, high-trust teams both adopt AI well and have managers who model its use)
  • The tasks AI can now automate (scheduling, drafting reviews, coordinating communication) are separable from the 'critical work' that actually determines whether AI investment pays off, so automating the former does not diminish the case for the latter
  • Rising scope, communication load, and direct-report counts documented by LeadDev represent additional value-generating managerial work rather than a sign of an already-strained role being asked to do more without more time or support
Inferences:
  • Never draws the inference that if flattening keeps widening spans of control, managers' capacity to perform the very behaviors credited with driving AI value (individualized modeling, psychological safety, quality standard-setting) may itself erode — the argument for 'managers matter more' doesn't reckon with flattening simultaneously reducing the time managers have per report
  • Never questions whether Microsoft (an AI-tooling vendor) and the Postman-sponsored LeadDev survey have a shared commercial interest in findings that argue for preserving manager seats who are also the ones who purchase and roll out enterprise AI tools

Narratives

  • The layoff wave at Meta, Google, and Amazon, and the extreme case of '50 ICs reporting into one supervisor' at Meta's applied-AI engineering reorganization
    → anchors the flattening narrative in concrete, high-profile examples before the essay pivots to rebut the logic behind them

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Is cutting managers because AI can perform their traditional tasks a sound basis for flattening?
    No — the essay argues managers' value now lies in translating AI investment into team-level results, not in the administrative tasks AI can automate.
  • ✅ Is the managerial role actually contracting under AI adoption?
    No — LeadDev's 2026 data shows scope, communication, team count, and direct reports increasing rather than shrinking.
  • ✅ Does manager behavior affect how successfully employees adopt and benefit from AI?
    Yes, per Microsoft's Work Trend Index — manager modeling and psychological safety correlate with large lifts in employee AI value, trust, and readiness.
  • ⭕ Are the widening scope, communication load, and report counts LeadDev documents a healthy sign of managers becoming more valuable, or a sign of an already-strained role being stretched further without added time or support?
    Not addressed — the essay treats the increases only as evidence against contraction, without asking whether they are sustainable.
  • ⭕ Will the managerial role eventually contract once AI adoption becomes the norm and managers no longer need to manage the change itself?
    Explicitly left open: 'We don't know how this will play out yet.'

Gaps

  • illogicalThe Microsoft Work Trend Index correlations (managers who model AI use and create psychological safety <-> higher employee AI value/trust) are treated as showing managers cause the AI-value-realization effect, but the reported data doesn't rule out reverse causation or a shared confound — e.g., managers who already lead high-functioning, high-trust teams may both model AI use more and see better AI outcomes for reasons unrelated to the modeling itself. This is the same causal-inference gap the corpus has repeatedly flagged for similar manager-effectiveness survey findings.
    Read next: Read against extract-lenny-noam-segal-tech-worker-ai-sentiment-2026#g1, which raises an identical causation-vs-selection concern about a structurally similar manager-effectiveness finding, for a methodologically stronger version of this claim (quasi-experimental studies of manager rotation/turnover on team AI outcomes).
  • incompleteThe essay never reconciles the tension its own evidence walks into: the LeadDev data it cites to show managers 'doing more' (more scope, more reports, more communication) is, elsewhere in this corpus, flagged as an unresolved structural contradiction — manager quality/attention is the single biggest lever on wellbeing, yet spans of control keep widening. If managers are stretched across more reports and more scope, their capacity to perform the very behaviors credited with driving AI value (individualized modeling, psychological safety, quality-standard setting) may itself erode, an inference the essay never draws.
    Read next: Reads as a partial answer to the reconciliation gap named in extract-lenny-noam-segal-tech-worker-ai-sentiment-2026#g2 and extract-tech-workers-2026-workforce-splitting-in-two#g4 (orgs flattening while manager quality is the biggest wellbeing lever) and extract-postman-engineering-leadership-report-2026#g5 (the same report's own reassurance-vs-accelerating-flattening tension) — but it only asserts a side (invest in/preserve managers) rather than explaining how broader spans and deeper manager investment can coexist.
  • uninformedBoth empirical sources cited — Microsoft's own Work Trend Index and the Postman-sponsored LeadDev Engineering Leadership Report — are produced or sponsored by parties with a commercial stake in enterprise AI seat and tool adoption (Microsoft Copilot; Postman's API platform, whose customers are the same engineering-leadership audience). The essay treats both as neutral evidence for 'invest in managers' without noting that a report emphasizing manager-mediated AI success also implicitly argues for retaining the manager seats who buy, roll out, and champion AI tooling.
    Read next: Compare to extract-postman-engineering-leadership-report-2026#g3, which flags the identical sponsored-study pattern for this same LeadDev report.
  • unaddressedThe essay rebuts the 'AI replaces manager tasks so cut managers' narrative in general terms ('managers are infrastructure') but never engages the sharper, competing claim already in circulation that AI eliminates specifically the *coordination* function managers historically performed (not managerial value generally) by letting ICs and PMs ship directly — a claim that argues for redefining, not simply preserving, the role.
    Read next: Read against extract-kill-your-org-chart-for-ai (management's purpose becomes developing judgment, not coordination) and extract-engineering-manager-role-splitting-in-two (the TLM/multi-team-EM fork) for competing accounts of which manager functions AI actually obsoletes versus elevates.
