Automatisert KI-koding vil endre alt ("Automated AI coding will change everything")

Henning Meese · article · 2026-01-09 source ↗ Reader 🖥️

AI's eventual takeover of most coding work is near-certain and the industry is already betting on it ('all in'), but the specific timeline keeps sliding later than predicted — illustrated by LessWrong's own 'superhuman coder' threshold estimate being pushed back 3.5 to 5 years in a single revision — so human developers are not about to become unemployed en masse in the near term, even though what role they will play once that threshold does arrive remains genuinely open.

theoretical · argumentative — A short Norwegian tech-outlet (Computerworld/cw.no) opinion piece, extracted and written up in English with quotations kept verbatim in Norwegian. Its central evidence is a specific external forecast (LessWrong's AI timelines/takeoff model) rather than primary research; the author's own argument sits on top of that forecast, reasoning about near-term developer employment given the forecast's own recent revision.

Key terms

  • overmenneskelig koder (superhuman coder)
    LessWrong's defined capability threshold: an AI system able to run 30 times as many agents as an organization has human developers, using only 5% of that organization's budget, operating autonomously at the level of one of the best human developers, and completing tasks 30 times faster than the organization's most skilled engineer.

Arguments

Large-scale developer unemployment is not imminent, even though AI will likely eventually make most coding an AI-performed job. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • The anticipated big productivity gains from AI — in coding specifically and AI generally — have repeatedly arrived later than first assumed.
  • LessWrong's own 'superhuman coder' threshold estimate was itself just revised 3.5-5 years later in a single update.
  • Software organizations still need the developers they currently employ, where they currently are.
Implicit premises:
  • A single forecasting community's (LessWrong's) revised estimate is a reasonable proxy for the industry's actual trajectory, even though that same community's prior estimate just proved substantially wrong in one direction.
  • The pattern of this one revision (pushed later) is representative, rather than forecasts being equally likely to be revised earlier in response to a future capability jump.
Inferences:
  • If AI capability-threshold forecasts keep being revised later on roughly this cadence, the 'not long now' framing could remain perpetually true without the threshold ever quite arriving on schedule — worth tracking as a recurring pattern in AI capability forecasting generally, not just in this one report.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Will AI automation of coding cause a large wave of developer unemployment in the near term?
    No — not in the near term, because the anticipated capability thresholds keep arriving later than predicted.
  • ⭕ What happens to deployed systems that nobody but the AI that built them understands, from a security/readiness standpoint?
  • ⭕ What role will human developers play once (or if) AI reaches a 'superhuman coder' threshold?
    Speculated as possibly strategy/control/oversight roles, but explicitly left uncertain.
