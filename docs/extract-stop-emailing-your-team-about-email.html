Stop emailing your team about email

Small law firms burn time emailing each other about client emails, and since forwarding, mailbox delegation and shared inboxes are all too blunt, they should try an AI email client (Slashy) whose per-person agents talk to each other (A2A), starting small with one teammate on low-stakes threads and after checking data-handling terms.

Key terms

  • emailing each other about the emails
    The coordination overhead where a team forwards or replies about a client message in a separate thread, detaching the discussion from the original message
  • A2A (agent-to-agent) Agent-to-Agent Protocols
    Each team member has their own AI agent and those agents communicate with each other, so teammates can discuss an email without emailing back and forth
  • delegate access
    Giving a colleague access to your whole mailbox, which grants far more than the few threads they need
  • AI email client
    An email application with an AI agent connected to inbox and calendar that does work (drafts, finds attachments, flags deadlines) and can be texted via iMessage/Slack

Arguments

Small law firms should try Slashy's agent-to-agent feature to cut email-about-email overhead. strength: low
Explicit premises:
  • Email forwarding/CC, delegated access and shared inboxes are each too blunt
  • Lawyers want thread-level sharing, adjacent private comments and AI legwork
  • Slashy connects an AI agent to inbox and calendar and supports A2A between teammates
Implicit premises:
  • A2A gives thread-scoped collaboration without handing over the inbox
  • No existing non-AI tool already provides shared comments on email threads
  • The vendor's text-message confirmation accurately describes the shipped feature
  • The author's enthusiasm is independent of his commercial relationship with the vendor
Inferences:
  • Per-person agents now hold standing access to each full mailbox, which moves the over-broad-permission problem from colleagues to the vendor
  • If A2A replaces human-composed internal email, a record of what agents told each other becomes a new thing to find, audit and possibly disclose
A conservative small firm can adopt such a tool safely by starting with one teammate on low-stakes threads and checking the data terms. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • Any connected tool sees client information
  • Low-stakes threads limit exposure during the trial
Implicit premises:
  • Reading a vendor's data terms is enough diligence for a tool that reads privileged email
  • Low-stakes threads can be separated reliably from confidential ones when the agent is connected to the whole inbox
Inferences:
  • Connecting the tool to one teammate's account may expose the whole mailbox including high-stakes threads, so 'low-stakes first' limits use, not access

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Is there a better way for a small team that lives in email to collaborate on emails than forwarding and replying?
    Yes: use an AI email client with agent-to-agent communication (Slashy), piloted cautiously.
  • ⭕ How can collaboration happen on an email without giving colleagues access to the whole mailbox?
    Implied answer is A2A, but the mechanism and its scoping are never explained.
  • ⭕ Is it professionally safe (confidentiality, privilege) to connect client email to a third-party AI agent?
    Deferred to the reader in one sentence: check how the company handles data.

Gaps

  • incompleteThe A2A mechanism is never described: what the agents exchange (summaries, whole threads, pointers), what permissions each agent holds, and whether a teammate's agent can see only the shared thread. The requirement the piece sets (share one thread, not the account) is exactly what a per-person agent with standing mailbox access may violate; the permission problem may simply move from colleague to vendor. Evidence for the feature is a vendor's brief text reply.
    Read next: Read agent-to-agent trust and authority-attenuation pieces (extract-the-interfaces-are-arriving, extract-google-agent-tools-interoperability-whitepaper) and a hands-on test of Slashy's sharing model.
  • incompleteConfidentiality is handled in a single caution sentence. For a profession bound by confidentiality duties, an AI agent reading privileged client email, and agents relaying content among themselves, is the central adoption question (retention, training use, subprocessors, privilege, bar-ethics guidance), not a footnote. Compare Svenson's much more developed consent and data-handling analysis for AI recorders.
    Read next: Read extract-svenson-two-ai-recorders alongside this post, and ethics opinions on lawyers using third-party AI on client email.
  • uninformedThe claim that the missing piece has been 'collaboration that happens where the email lives' is not checked against existing non-AI tools for team inboxes (for example shared-inbox or collaborative email clients with internal comments on threads, as far as I know from general knowledge rather than the corpus). The piece contrasts Slashy only with plain Gmail delegation and forwarding, which makes the AI/A2A layer look more necessary than it may be.
    Read next: Survey collaborative email clients and their thread-level comment and sharing features before concluding AI is the missing ingredient.
  • incompleteThe commercial relationship is only partly visible: the author loves the product, the vendor spoke to his paid Inner Circle group, and a P.S. promotes a follow-up session, but there is no statement of whether he is paid, sponsored, or an affiliate. As with his earlier Viktor post, a newsletter endorsement is used as a recommendation for a general audience without independent verification of the feature claims.
