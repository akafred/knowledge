As AI makes intelligence and agency abundant, the scarce human contribution becomes vision and ambition — a clear view of the future you want to push the world toward — and because AI makes execution easier, you should dream bigger.

practical · expository — A ~108-word X/Twitter post by Y Combinator quoting three short passages from Alexandr Wang's talk at Startup School 2026 (July 29th). It is a short excerpt of a longer talk/interview; the original conversation is not captured in Reader, so only bare assertions are available, with no argument, evidence, or context. Practical in intent (what founders should cultivate), but essentially a pull-quote.

Key terms

  • vision
    A clear view of what you want the world to look like in the future, and the one way you want to influence its development.
  • ambition
    The scale of what you are willing to attempt; rises as AI lowers the cost of execution ('dream bigger').
  • agency Agency
    The capacity to act and get things done, which Wang counts as something that will no longer be scarce (AI supplies it).
  • put your finger on the scale
    Deliberately choosing one way to tilt how the future develops.

Arguments

People should invest in cultivating a clear vision and bigger ambition rather than in raw intelligence or capacity to act.
Explicit premises:
  • Intelligence and agency will not be the scarce resource.
  • AI makes execution easier.
Implicit premises:
  • Scarcity is what confers value, so whatever stays scarce is where human advantage lies.
  • AI will not itself generate vision or ambition (or at least not in a way that substitutes for the human's).
  • Vision is a distinct capacity from intelligence, so abundant intelligence does not automatically yield it.
Inferences:
  • If the binding constraint is knowing what to build, execution-focused skills and org structures lose relative value to direction-setting ones.
  • If many people can dream bigger at once, scarcity may shift again toward distribution, attention, or coordination.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ What remains valuable and scarce for humans when intelligence and the ability to act are abundant?
    Vision and ambition: knowing what future you want and aiming at something large.
  • ⭕ What distinguishes visionary from merely capable people (what is 'vision' concretely, and how is it developed)?
    Only a diagnostic question is offered; no account of how to form or test a vision.

Gaps

  • incompleteThe excerpt never argues why vision and ambition will stay scarce while intelligence and agency become abundant. It does not distinguish vision from taste or judgment, or say why a sufficiently capable AI could not propose or pursue goals; the scarcity claim is a bare assertion by someone whose company and career are built on AI abundance.
