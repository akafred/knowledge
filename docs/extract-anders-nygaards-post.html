practical · mixed — Opinion/advocacy LinkedIn post (in Norwegian) with a personal anecdote. It argues that organizations should not default to Microsoft Copilot, and uses a self-described showcase pipeline to demonstrate what the author's preferred stack (Claude plus agents) can do. The evidence is anecdotal (a single self-reported project in a hobby niche, with no figures on cost, quality or comparison against Copilot), and the author is openly a Claude-user advocate with a talk to promote. Treat it as a stance and a provocation rather than as an evaluation.

Key terms

  • moat (økonomisk vollgrav) Moats & Defensibility
    Copilot as a defensive barrier protecting Microsoft's Office franchise (a bundled lock-in product) rather than a tool whose value stands on its own to the user.
  • AI-reisen på andre klasse (the AI journey in second class)
    Taking up AI with a weaker tool and shallower practice, often unaware of what is being missed.
  • trakt (funnel)
    The shape of the AI understanding gap: a broad mouth of people who have barely tried AI, a middle of professionals with moderate gains, and a narrow stem of people for whom the curve goes straight up, all coexisting.
  • OKF bundle
    The 100MB distilled knowledge package produced from 10GB of scraped material, used as the basis for the book. The post does not expand the acronym (possibly an 'open knowledge format'; unconfirmed).
  • feedbackloopen er kort (the feedback loop is short)
    Proofreading by listening to the audiobook and logging changes in Linear on a phone, which an agent picks up and applies immediately, so editing and revision form one continuous loop.

Arguments

Defaulting to Copilot licences means taking the AI journey second class. strength: low
Explicit premises:
  • Copilot looks more like a Microsoft moat around Office than a real productivity tool.
  • Boards delegate AI to IT, and IT buys Copilot licences because Claude is perceived as more complicated on compliance and training.
  • The author's Claude-based pipeline achieves things he challenges others to reproduce in Copilot.
Implicit premises:
  • The relevant capability difference is between the products, not between the depth of use of the people using them.
  • Compliance and training costs for Claude are small or surmountable relative to the capability gain.
  • A heavy, bespoke agentic project in a hobby domain is a fair proxy for the value Copilot would or would not give typical office workers.
  • The author's experience of Copilot is first-hand and current; nothing in the post says so.
Inferences:
  • If organizations choose tools on perceived compliance simplicity instead of capability, their AI maturity is capped by procurement convenience.
  • A vendor that bundles AI with existing licences may win through distribution rather than merit, so adoption statistics for Copilot say little about its value.
AI discussions are strange because people occupy different positions in a widening funnel of experience. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • Karpathy says the gap in understanding of what AI can do is growing.
  • Roughly 6 billion have barely tried it, most professionals see moderate gain, and a few see exponential improvement.
  • These groups coexist at the same time.
Implicit premises:
  • The people who report the steepest gains are seeing reality more accurately than the moderate-gain majority, and not merely selecting or framing differently.
  • The experience gap is explained mainly by use depth and tooling, not by task fit or domain.
Inferences:
  • Hype and denial coexist because each camp is reporting its own, genuine, local experience.
  • Improving shared understanding requires exposure to deep use, not argument.

Narratives

  • The Paradox sailboat project: the author scraped hundreds of sources, recreated defunct forums via the Wayback Machine API, pulled sailing magazines from archive.org, ran about 300 hours of compute, compressed 10GB to a 100MB bundle, wrote a book, recorded an ElevenLabs audiobook, proofread by listening and logging edits in Linear, and builds HTML decks for the talk.
    → Shows what the 'narrow top of the funnel' looks like in practice and implicitly supports the claim that Claude-with-agents delivers something Copilot users are not even aware of.
  • The boardroom-to-licence story: a board says the company must invest in AI, IT is put on the case, and the default answer is to buy Copilot licences because Claude looks harder to govern and train.
    → Illustrates the procurement path that produces second-class adoption and supports the 'moat' claim by showing Copilot winning through convenience and bundling.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Why do so many Norwegian organizations choose Copilot for their AI push?
    Because boards delegate to IT, Copilot is the easy licence to buy, and Claude is perceived as harder on compliance and training. The explanation is asserted from experience, not evidenced.
  • ⭕ Is Copilot actually useful for knowledge work?
    The author asserts it is more moat than tool and challenges others to show otherwise, but never reports a direct trial or comparison.
  • ✅ Why do AI discussions feel so incoherent?
    People occupy different positions in a widening funnel and have literally seen different things (via Karpathy).
  • ⭕ Are the compliance and training concerns about Claude genuine?
    Treated as perception only ('oppleves'); the post does not test or answer them.

Gaps

  • incompleteNo actual Copilot comparison is offered. The post never says which Copilot (Microsoft 365 Copilot, GitHub Copilot, Copilot Cowork, which is itself built on Claude), what the author tried, or when. 'Show me that you have done the same in Copilot' is a rhetorical challenge, not evidence, and shifts the burden of proof without testing the claim.
    Read next: Look for side-by-side or practitioner comparisons of M365 Copilot and Claude for knowledge work; compare the Coop Bank Copilot-replacement post and the Anthropic-vs-Microsoft Copilot Cowork discussion in this corpus.
  • illogicalThe post blends two different claims: that the AI understanding gap is a funnel of engagement depth (Karpathy), and that the choice of vendor puts organizations on the wrong side of that funnel. A power user building a bespoke pipeline might reach the narrow end with any tool, and a Claude licence in a mandated, untrained organization may sit at the broad end. Selection effects (the author is a motivated, skilled builder) are never considered.
    Read next: Evidence on whether AI outcomes track tool choice or user skill and practice (for example the junior-employee amplifier/delegator/apprentice study and the DORA AI capabilities model in this corpus).
  • incompleteCompliance and training are dismissed as perceived complexity, but for Norwegian organizations they include real issues (data residency, tenant integration, GDPR/Datatilsynet stance, procurement frameworks, M365 grounding in SharePoint and email). The post does not say what a compliant Claude rollout would look like or what it would cost.
    Read next: Norwegian public and private examples of compliant Claude or open-weight rollouts; the Coop Bank Copilot-replacement post; Datatilsynet guidance on AI assistants.
  • incompleteCategory mismatch and no outcome data in the anecdote. A niche hobby corpus turned into a book and audiobook through a bespoke, high-effort pipeline is not the workload Copilot is bought for (email, documents, meetings, spreadsheets). The post gives no cost, no accuracy or quality check of the scraped and distilled material, and no word on copyright or licensing of the scraped forums and magazines.
    Read next: Measured outcome studies of agentic knowledge-base pipelines (for example Karpathy-style LLM wiki write-ups and their maintenance costs) and discussions of how to evaluate distilled knowledge bundles.
  • uninformedThe Karpathy source is a shortened link and the funnel figures (about 6 billion barely tried, most professionals moderate, a few exponential) are unsourced; it is unclear which parts are Karpathy's words and which are the author's gloss. The OKF bundle is also undefined, so the technical core of the demonstration cannot be checked.
