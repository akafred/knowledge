Key terms

  • felles praksis (shared/common practice)
    The team-level, jointly-worked-out way of using AI agents — as distinct from the sum of individual team members' own habits. Includes agreed task-specification conventions, guardrails, verification routines, and shared judgment about when agents add value. Something the team has to build together, not something that emerges automatically once individuals are proficient.
  • paradokset (the paradox)
    That teams seriously trying to extract value from AI may initially slow down, because the visible speed gain (faster code) is not where the real bottleneck or the real payoff sits — those are in specification, verification, and shared practice formation.

Arguments

Teams that genuinely pursue value from AI-assisted coding may work slower at first, because the real productivity gain requires the team to develop a new shared way of working, not just faster code generation. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • AI accelerates code writing, but coding is only one part of the development process (p1).
  • Faster coding shifts work toward task specification and quality verification (p2).
  • Moving from individual AI use to shared team practice takes time (p3).
  • The team-level work — process change, task specification, guardrails, judging agent value, deciding where to experiment — is harder and slower than individual adoption (p4).
  • Closing the gap requires the team to experiment and learn together (p5).
Implicit premises:
  • Team-level practice change is necessarily slower than individual habit formation.
  • Organizational/team AI value is gated by the team's slowest-forming collective practice, not by individual tool proficiency.
Inferences:
  • Leaders and teams should expect and budget for an initial slowdown as a sign of genuine engagement with AI, not as a failure signal.
  • Measuring AI's value purely by code-writing speed will miss where the real bottleneck — and the real payoff — actually sit.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Why doesn't AI's coding speed translate into proportionally faster teams?
    Because code-writing is only one part of development; the freed-up time shifts to task specification and quality verification, and because moving from individual AI use to a shared team practice — process change, specification conventions, guardrails, and judgment about when agents help — takes deliberate joint work, not just faster tooling.
