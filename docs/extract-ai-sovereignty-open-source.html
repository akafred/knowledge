AI Sovereignty: Bargaining with Big Tech and the Promise of Full Stack Open Source AI

Nicole Butterfield · rss · 2026-09-22 Reader 🖥️

Agent HarnessAgent-to-Agent ProtocolsAI Regulation & Governance (External)Compute & Energy InfrastructureData SovereigntyMCP (Model Context Protocol)Open-Weight Models
Genuine AI sovereignty requires building and sustaining an interoperable, full-stack open-source AI ecosystem — spanning agent harnesses, inference engines, protocols, data stores, and observability tooling, not just open-weight models or vendor-branded 'sovereignty' offerings — because dependency and acquisition pressure persist at every layer that stays closed or commercially controlled.

practical · expository — Argues for a course of action (institutions and the open-source community should build/adopt full-stack open AI infrastructure) rather than merely describing the sovereignty landscape; structured as a survey of stack layers in service of that recommendation.

Key terms

  • AI sovereignty
    Not total control of one's AI stack, but having enough localized security/privacy, jurisdictional compliance, service reliability, and cultural fit — plus enough alternatives beyond commercial offerings — to negotiate interdependence from a position of leverage rather than being locked in.
  • architecture of participation
    (Quoting Tim O'Reilly) A federated system — of models, protocols/code, and capacity — designed so that both 'being everywhere' and 'everyone having a say' are structurally possible, as opposed to a single dominant stack.
  • agent harness Agent Harness
    The orchestration layer sitting above model weights, cited (via Mozilla CTO Raffi Krikorian) as where capability is now concentrating and which closed labs are 'welding shut' — making open harnesses (not just open weights) a key sovereignty battleground.

Arguments

Genuine AI sovereignty requires a federated, full-stack open-source ecosystem, not just open-weight models or vendor-branded 'sovereignty' offerings. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • Big Tech's own sovereignty offerings still deepen U.S. dependency (per HAI report).
  • Open-weight models alone leave the harness, inference, protocol, storage, and observability layers under commercial or closed control.
  • Open infrastructure projects already exist across most layers (vLLM, Ray, Ollama, MCP/A2A, Qdrant, MLflow, etc.), but resiliency gaps remain in storage and observability (per AI Potluck Gap Map).
Implicit premises:
  • Sovereignty is fundamentally about maintaining genuine choice and negotiating leverage, not about eliminating dependency entirely.
  • A single closed or immature layer in the stack can recreate a chokepoint even when every other layer is open.
Inferences:
  • Because storage and observability remain the least mature open layers, they are the most likely near-term battleground for further commercial acquisition — a prediction the piece's own examples (Hugging Face, OpenRouter) already partly illustrate at the model-routing/hub layer.

Narratives

  • DeepSeek's 2025 release prompted claims of censorship and control in Chinese models; more recently, probes have examined how US models may also limit certain discourse.
    → Implicitly supports the proposition that political-control risk is not unique to one geopolitical bloc — both US-dependency and non-US alternative-dependency carry it.
  • Stripe's acquisition of OpenRouter, NVIDIA's acquisition of Hugging Face, and NVIDIA's licensing deal with Poolside ("purportedly to avoid the oversight of complete acquisition")
    → Supports the proposition that open-source AI infrastructure remains persistently vulnerable to commercial absorption, and grounds the piece's closing warning in concrete, near-term examples.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ What does AI sovereignty actually require, beyond simply adopting open-weight models?
    A federated, full-stack open infrastructure spanning agent harnesses, inference engines, protocols, data/knowledge stores, and observability/evaluation tooling.
  • ✅ Which layers of the open-source AI stack are currently underserved?
    Storage and observability, per the AI Potluck Gap Map, versus more mature inference/deployment/agent-protocol ecosystems.
  • ⭕ How much interdependence should an institution accept, and by what criteria should it 'calibrate' that interdependence?
