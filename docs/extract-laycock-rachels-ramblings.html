Why I'm Writing Rachel's Ramblings

Rachel Laycock (Thoughtworks CTO; martinfowler.com — Rachel's Ramblings) · rss · 2026-07-28 source ↗ Reader 🖥️

Writing as Thinking
Rachel Laycock has real, not-yet-articulated patterns and hypotheses about how software, teams, and organizations are changing in the AI era, and she is committing to publish them fast and imperfectly — rather than polished and delayed — because writing is the forcing function that turns her associative, conversation-honed thinking into something durable outside her head.

practical · narrative — A short, personal launch post for a new blog, not a technical argument about software engineering itself. Its only substantive claim is about why and how she will write going forward; the actual team/org/AI content is deferred to future posts (one of which, 'The Conductor Developer,' is already in this corpus).

Arguments

Publishing her pattern-recognition fast and imperfectly is a better use of it than continuing to withhold it until polished.
Explicit premises:
  • She has real, not-yet-articulated patterns and hypotheses that leaders could act on.
  • The barrier to sharing them has been self-imposed (overthinking, pace, polish standard), not a lack of capacity or material.
  • Writing is a forcing function that turns tacit, conversational pattern-matching into an examinable, durable artifact.
Implicit premises:
  • An early, imperfect articulation of a pattern is more valuable to readers than withholding it until it's fully formed and possibly outdated — i.e., the cost of occasionally being wrong or evolving in public is lower than the cost of staying silent.
  • Her associative, knowledge-graph-style thinking translates adequately into linear written prose without losing whatever makes it valuable in live conversation and client work.

Narratives

  • At the Future of Software conference, surrounded by people sharing their latest ideas, Laycock had the 'slightly uncomfortable realization' that she has her own patterns and hypotheses that she simply hasn't been writing down.
    → Supplies the concrete, dateable trigger for the entire post's thesis ('I have ideas. I haven't been writing them. That's about to change.') rather than presenting the decision as an abstract resolution.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Why hasn't she been publishing her thinking despite having real, industry-relevant patterns to share?
    Three self-imposed habits: overthinking, moving on too fast, and an inflated polish bar — not busyness.
  • ✅ What will this new blog actually be about?
    Four stated areas: the future of software; AI's effect on software development for engineers/leaders/organizations; how platforms, agents, and people work together; occasional personal/work-life notes.
  • ⭕ Will the fast/imperfect format actually solve the specific habit of moving on before finishing an idea, which she names as one of her three blockers?

Gaps

  • incompleteThe post diagnoses three blocking habits (overthinking, moving too fast to the next idea, excess polish) but proposes one fix (write fast and imperfectly) that addresses the first and third directly while leaving the second — abandoning ideas before finishing them — essentially unaddressed; a 'fast and imperfect' format could just as easily amplify that habit (more half-finished fragments) as cure it.
