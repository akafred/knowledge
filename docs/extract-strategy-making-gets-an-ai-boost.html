Key terms

  • bounded rationality
    As cited from Felipe Csaszar: 'the idea that human decision-makers, however capable, are constrained by finite attention, memory, and processing power' — the reason executive teams have historically needed simplifying strategy tools (SWOT, growth-share matrices, Porter's five forces).
  • unbound (strategy-making is unbound)
    Gavett's own framing word for what happens once AI removes bounded-rationality constraints: strategy-making is freed to consider far more alternatives, richer models, and less groupthink-distorted deliberation than a human team alone could sustain.
  • calm Evolutionary Architecture & Fitness Functions
    As Gratton uses it (quoted): 'the mental space needed to exercise good judgement' — a deliberately protected, practiced state, not passive relaxation, that lets someone navigate uncertainty and pressure.

Arguments

Senior leaders should actively use AI in strategy-making — to widen strategic options, replace static snapshots with dynamic models, design rigorous tests for ideas, and redefine the strategist's role from analyst to architect — rather than continue relying solely on legacy simplification frameworks. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • Human strategists are bounded-rational: constrained by finite attention, memory, and processing power
  • AI is not similarly bounded: it can generate/screen thousands of alternatives, build continuously updated dynamic models, and run simulated deliberation without groupthink, hierarchy, or time pressure
Implicit premises:
  • Removing a cognitive/structural constraint on strategic-option generation produces objectively better strategic outcomes, independent of whether the resulting larger option set can actually be evaluated well by a human team operating under the same bounded-rationality limits that motivated the argument in the first place
  • The 'questions only humans can answer' (what do we believe, what are we willing to risk) are cleanly separable from the option-generation and modeling work being handed to AI, rather than themselves requiring the kind of broad option-scanning AI is being credited with
Inferences:
  • If AI can generate a near-limitless set of strategic alternatives, the scarce resource shifts from option generation to option evaluation and selection — a capacity this argument doesn't address, and one that plausibly reintroduces the same bounded-rationality problem AI was meant to solve, just one step downstream

Narratives

Questions addressed

  • ✅ How should senior leaders use AI in strategy-making, given the cognitive limits of legacy frameworks?
    Widen strategic options before narrowing them, replace static snapshots with dynamic models, design rigorous ways to test ideas, and redefine the strategist's role from analyst to architect.
  • ✅ How should leaders navigate a career that is simultaneously lengthening and losing its predictable shape?
    Three rules: prepare before you need to, commit to what lasts, and protect time for judgment.
  • ⭕ If AI removes the bottleneck on generating strategic options, what removes the bottleneck on evaluating and choosing among them?
