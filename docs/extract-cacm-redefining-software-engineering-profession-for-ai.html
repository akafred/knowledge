Redefining the Software Engineering Profession for AI

Mark Russinovich and Scott Hanselman · article · 2026-10-08 source ↗ Reader 🖥️

Career DevelopmentCognitive DebtJunior Hiring EconomicsSystemic Apprenticeship & Skill TransmissionTalent Density
Because generative AI amplifies senior engineering judgment while penalizing early-in-career developers, organizations must deliberately keep hiring and investing in EiC engineers through a structured 'preceptorship at scale' system -- senior mentors paired with AI tooling designed to teach, not just produce -- or the profession's talent pipeline will collapse.

practical · argumentative — Opens with a diagnosis (seniority-biased technological change is collapsing the junior pipeline) but the thesis is explicitly a call to action: organizations must keep hiring early-in-career (EiC) engineers and build a structured 'preceptorship' system around them.

Key terms

  • AI boost / AI drag
    The split outcome of agentic coding assistants: a multiplicative productivity gain for senior engineers who can steer and verify AI output ('boost'), versus a net negative effect on early-in-career developers who lack the judgment to do so ('drag').
  • EiC (early-in-career) developer
    A deliberately chosen replacement for 'junior,' denoting engineers early in their career trajectory rather than low-status or low-value -- the article is explicit that the word choice matters for how these developers are treated.
  • Narrowing pyramid hypothesis
    The claim that generative AI acts as seniority-biased technological change, shrinking the base of the traditional EiC:lead staffing pyramid (commonly ~10:1) because organizations stop hiring/training the base while AI amplifies the apex.
  • Preceptorship at scale
    A formal, accountable mentorship structure (borrowed from medical/nursing training) in which trained senior 'preceptors' (each covering 3-5 EiC developers) are explicitly responsible for mentoring outcomes, not just throughput, aided by AI tooling that surfaces reasoning and teachable moments.
  • EiC mode
    A proposed explicit mode for coding assistants that defaults to Socratic coaching before code generation -- challenging the learner, quizzing on key decisions, tracking strengths/weaknesses -- analogous to Khan Academy's Khanmigo, rather than generating a finished solution immediately.

Arguments

Organizations should fund a formal preceptor program rather than rely on ad hoc mentoring or hope AI alone will train new engineers. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • Generative AI amplifies senior judgment but penalizes developers who lack it.
  • Without deliberate hiring and investment, EiC developers will not accumulate the 'systems taste' that makes seniors valuable.
  • A structured program with explicit ratios, duration, and accountability is more likely to produce consistent outcomes than informal mentorship.
Implicit premises:
  • Senior engineers are willing and able to take on preceptor duties without those duties being treated as a pure tax on their own AI-boosted output.
  • Organizations will tolerate near-term capacity reduction for a payoff that is long-term and partly external to the firm (an industry-wide talent supply).
Inferences:
  • If preceptor duties are not explicitly resourced and rewarded (separate from individual output metrics), the program likely collapses under the same short-term incentive pressures that caused the junior-hiring collapse in the first place -- a tension the article does not fully resolve.
Coding assistants themselves should be redesigned with an EiC-specific teaching mode rather than treated as a uniform tool for all skill levels. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • Unmediated AI use lets EiC developers skip the struggle that builds judgment (via the Mollick quotations).
  • Direct engagement produces better learning outcomes than AI-mediated shortcutting (via the MIT cognitive-debt study).
Implicit premises:
  • EiC developers and their managers will actually choose to enable a mode that slows them down in the short term, rather than defaulting to the fastest path to a shipped PR.
  • Socratic coaching generalizes from math/science tutoring (Khanmigo) to the much more open-ended domain of production software engineering.
Inferences:
  • If EiC mode is opt-in, the developers most in need of it (those under delivery pressure) are the least likely to enable it -- an adoption problem the article doesn't address.

Narratives

  • Microsoft's Project Societas (the Office Agent): seven part-time engineers delivered a consumer-ready preview in 10 weeks, producing 110,000+ lines of code that was 98% AI-generated, with human work shifting from authoring to directing.
    → Shows the 'AI boost' side of the asymmetry concretely -- what senior-engineer-directed agentic work can achieve at scale.
  • The Aspire project's phased evolution from local chat assistants, to agents opening PRs, to full 'human-agent swarms' where every PR is shippable and review is a shared human-machine dialogue.
    → Illustrates how agentic workflows mature over time, reinforcing that the 'boost' requires senior engineers steering architectural goals while agents execute.
  • A coding agent inserts a sleep() call to mask a crashing race condition, cannot coherently explain its own reasoning when challenged, and admits its reasoning was flawed -- yet an EiC developer might have accepted the fix as effective because the symptom (crash) disappeared.
    → Carries the article's central claim about judgment without having to argue abstractly for it: shows exactly why EiC developers specifically (not engineers in general) are vulnerable to AI's superficially convincing failures.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ How should organizations respond to generative AI disproportionately amplifying senior engineers while penalizing early-in-career ones?
    Keep hiring EiC developers deliberately, accept short-term capacity reduction, and build a formal preceptorship system (senior mentors + AI tooling designed to teach) around them.
  • ⭕ Who bears the cost of preceptorship, and how is that cost sustained against the same short-term financial pressure that caused the junior-hiring collapse in the first place?
  • ⭕ Does an EiC-mode, Socratic-coaching coding assistant actually improve engineering judgment, or only essay-writing-style recall (the only domain the cited MIT study measured)?

Gaps

  • incompleteThe article proposes 3:1-5:1 preceptor ratios, a preceptor-training subset of seniors, and chat-log review, but gives no account of how preceptor duties are resourced against individual performance metrics, promotion criteria, or the same AI-boosted throughput expectations now applied to seniors -- the exact incentive misalignment the article diagnoses as the cause of the junior-hiring collapse.
    Read next: Hanselman's own earlier remarks (in extract-hanselman-training-developers-ai-routine-work) go further on this specific point -- proposing that preceptor performance be evaluated on how well they mint new seniors, not on their own output -- which this ACM piece doesn't restate.
  • incompleteThe MIT 'cognitive debt' study (Kosmyna et al.) measured essay-writing with ChatGPT, not software engineering with coding agents; the article extrapolates its learning-outcomes finding to justify the EiC-mode / Socratic-coaching proposal without evidence from the actual domain.
    Read next: Empirical study of whether Socratic-mode coding assistants (if any exist) actually improve junior engineers' systems judgment over time, versus simply slowing them down.
  • uninformedThe article treats the Massoum/Lichtinger seniority-biased-technological-change finding (13% drop in 22-25-year-old AI-exposed-job employment) as settled evidence of AI causation, without engaging competing explanations (e.g. a broader post-ZIRP hiring slowdown, return-to-office effects, or general new-grad market softness that predates widespread agentic coding adoption).
    Read next: extract-dora-2025-state-of-ai-assisted-software-development and extract-faros-acceleration-whiplash both discuss AI's effect on junior engineers with more methodological caution and are worth reading against this claim.
