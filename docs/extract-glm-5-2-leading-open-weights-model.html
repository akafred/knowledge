GLM-5.2 is the new leading open weights model on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index

Z.ai's GLM-5.2 is now the leading open-weights model on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index v4.1 and the Pareto frontier of intelligence versus cost per task, matching proprietary frontier models on real-world agentic work (GDPval-AA v2) despite being markedly less token-efficient than its open-weight peers.

theoretical · expository — A data report: it asserts what is true about GLM-5.2's relative standing on named benchmarks, backed by numbers and charts, rather than arguing the reader should do something. The practical upshot (which model to deploy) is left implicit in the ranking and Pareto-frontier framing.

Key terms

  • Intelligence Index v4.1
    Artificial Analysis's composite benchmark score aggregating multiple evals (including CritPt, HLE, AA-LCR, tau3, SciCode, TerminalBench, GPQA Diamond) into one number; v4.1 is a newly revised version 'featuring upgraded benchmarks and new per-task metrics' per AA's own companion article, shifting toward agentic workloads.
  • GDPval-AA v2
    AA's benchmark for real-world, agentic knowledge-work tasks, scored as an Elo rating baselined so that human performance = 1000, judged by a rotating panel of frontier-model judges, with agent turn limits raised from 100 to 250 versus the prior version.
  • Pareto frontier (of Intelligence vs Cost per Task)
    The set of models that are not dominated by any other model on both axes simultaneously — i.e., no other model is both as intelligent and cheaper. AA credits GLM-5.2 with the lowest cost per task among models 'at its intelligence level.'
  • Active parameters AI Unit Economics
    The subset of a mixture-of-experts model's total parameters (40B of 744B) actually used per token/forward pass — used here as the comparability anchor between GLM-5.1 and GLM-5.2 ('same size... but scores 11 points higher').

Arguments

GLM-5.2 is now competitive with top proprietary models on real-world knowledge work, not just with other open-weight models. strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • GLM-5.2 scores 1524 on GDPval-AA v2, essentially level with GPT-5.5 (xhigh reasoning) at 1514
  • AA states it visually inspected GLM-5.2's outputs across a range of GDPval-AA tasks and attached a selection
Implicit premises:
  • GDPval-AA v2's Elo score — human-baselined and judged by a rotating panel of frontier models — is a valid, unbiased proxy for real-world knowledge-work quality
  • A ~10-point Elo gap (1524 vs 1514) is within noise / effectively a tie rather than a meaningful gap
Inferences:
  • If open and closed frontier models have converged on real-world task quality, cost per task rather than raw capability becomes the deciding factor in model selection
GLM-5.2 offers the best intelligence-per-dollar among current open-weight models.
Explicit premises:
  • GLM-5.2 sits on the Pareto frontier of Intelligence vs Cost per Task
  • Its ~$0.46 per-task cost is the lowest among models at its intelligence level
Implicit premises:
  • AA's 'cost per task' figure already incorporates GLM-5.2's higher token spend (43k/task) at GLM-5.2's specific per-token price, so the verbosity finding and the low-cost finding are compatible rather than contradictory
  • AA's first-party API pricing snapshot is representative of what most buyers will actually pay, despite a fast-moving, heavily discounted third-party open-weight hosting market (DeepInfra, Novita, Nebius, etc.)
Inferences:
  • Cost-per-task and token-efficiency are not interchangeable metrics: a model can be simultaneously the least token-efficient and the cheapest per task, if its per-token price is low enough

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Which open-weights model currently leads on general intelligence benchmarks?
    GLM-5.2, at Intelligence Index v4.1 score 51.
  • ✅ Can an open-weights model match proprietary frontier models on real-world knowledge work?
    Yes, per GDPval-AA v2 — GLM-5.2 (1524) is roughly level with GPT-5.5 xhigh (1514).
  • ⭕ Does GLM-5.2's higher token verbosity erode its cost advantage?
    Not directly reconciled — the article reports higher per-task token use and the lowest cost per task at its intelligence level side by side, without explaining how the two are compatible (implicitly: low per-token pricing more than offsets the extra tokens).

Gaps

  • illogicalThe article's two headline benchmarks — Intelligence Index v4.1 and GDPval-AA v2 — both received major methodology revisions in the same release cycle as GLM-5.2 itself (v4.1 is described elsewhere by AA as 'a shift toward agentic workloads, featuring upgraded benchmarks and new per-task metrics'; GDPval-AA v2 rebaselines Elo, adds a rotating judge panel, and nearly triples the turn limit). The article never states whether the comparison figures for GLM-5.1, MiniMax-M3, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Kimi K2.6 and GPT-5.5 were rescored under the new methodology or carried over from the old one, so 'GLM-5.2 gained 11 points' and 'GLM-5.2 is now level with GPT-5.5' cannot be cleanly attributed to the model versus the yardstick that measures it.
    Read next: Read against extract-almedra-token-efficiency-benchmark-glm-5-2, which flags an analogous single-run/no-baseline problem for a different GLM-5.2 benchmark; look for AA's own methodology writeup on Intelligence Index v4.1 to see if prior-generation models were re-run under it.
  • incompleteNo variance, repetition, or error-bar reporting for any of the headline numbers (score 51 vs 44, GDPval-AA 1524 vs 1514, cost ~$0.46/task) — every comparison is a single point estimate, so it's unclear whether a 1-3 point or $0.01-0.05 gap is a real capability/cost difference or within run-to-run noise. The article also gives no detail on how 'cost per task' is computed (which pricing tier, cached vs uncached tokens, which task set).
    Read next: Compare with the Almedra benchmark's own gap about undocumented statistical procedures, and with the corpus's recurring caution (extract-ai-models-disappear-overnight) that industry benchmarks are useful for orientation, not as a substitute for task-specific evals.
  • uninformedThe article does not disclose or discuss that Artificial Analysis is both the designer of the benchmarks (Intelligence Index, GDPval-AA) and the sole publisher of the resulting rankings, with no independent replication cited. It also says nothing about what GLM-5.2's release — an MIT-licensed, commercially usable, frontier-capability open-weights model — means for safety or governance, despite the corpus already flagging this exact silence for GLM-5.1.
