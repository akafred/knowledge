An LLM wiki changed how I work

Casey Newton · rss · 2026-08-19 source ↗ Reader 🖥️

Agent MemoryKnowledge BaseRetrieval & RAGVibe CodingAI Workflow Patterns
Building a Karpathy-style, LLM-compiled and LLM-maintained personal wiki — seeded with his own multi-year reporting archive and fed daily by his reading — has been the single most useful change Casey Newton made to his work this year, replacing two earlier, effortful manual knowledge-tracking practices (Notion's agentic link search and Capacities' hand-curated 'blips') with a self-organizing but high-maintenance external memory system that he cannot yet confidently recommend to anyone else.

practical · mixed — Primarily a first-person narrative/expository account of Newton's annual productivity-tool review, organized around a practical verdict (what to keep doing, what to stop, what's new) rather than a theoretical claim; the LLM-wiki section carries a light argumentative thread about whether the system is worth its maintenance cost and whether it can be recommended to others. The trailing 'Following' news-roundup section (OpenAI safety changes, Meta addiction trial) is a separate newsletter segment not analyzed here, since it is unrelated in subject to the titled essay.

Key terms

  • LLM wiki
    A folder of interlinked Markdown files, compiled and continuously updated by an LLM from source documents (Newton's own archive plus daily-clipped reading), browsable in Obsidian and queryable via a plugin (Claudian) — following the process Andrej Karpathy described.
  • infohazard
    An idea so immediately compelling on exposure that simply learning of it commits the hearer to weeks of costly follow-through, with no guarantee of payoff — used self-mockingly about his own reaction to Karpathy's tweet.
  • blips
    Newton's own term (credited to Andy Matuschak's influence) for hand-created Capacities pages that gather links and notes around an emerging, not-yet-fully-formed story or topic over time, to be revisited later.

Arguments

The LLM wiki is worth its maintenance burden, at least for someone whose work resembles Newton's (research-heavy, requiring reconstruction of complex, evolving stories under deadline). strength: medium
Explicit premises:
  • During the fast-evolving, multi-week OpenAI/Hugging Face agentic-breach story, pulling up the wiki page each day to refresh his memory before writing, podcasting, or appearing on other podcasts 'saved [him] tons of time.'
Implicit premises:
  • The time cost of building and maintaining the wiki pipeline is smaller than the time it saves during high-complexity, multi-week stories.
  • Verifying an already-drafted answer against original sources is cheaper than researching a complex story from scratch each time it needs to be revisited.
Inferences:
  • If the payoff scales with the frequency of extended, multi-threaded stories in a given beat, the wiki's return on effort may be much lower for readers or workers whose subject matter doesn't feature this kind of story.
Newton cannot confidently recommend the LLM wiki to others, even though it was personally his most valuable change this year. strength: high
Explicit premises:
  • The system 'seems too specific to me and too clunky.'
  • It needs 'more or less constant maintenance': pages growing too long, silent pipeline failures, and a house-style problem that had to be fixed by bringing in a second LLM.
Implicit premises:
  • A system's fit to one very particular workflow and tolerance for ongoing tinkering does not establish its fitness for a general reader.
Inferences:
  • A packaged product version of this workflow (which Newton gestures at via the Karpathy 'idea file' framing and the Town/Greze interview) would need to absorb this maintenance burden before it could become broadly recommendable.

Narratives

  • Using the wiki daily during the multi-week OpenAI/Hugging Face agentic-breach story to refresh his memory of a complicated, fast-evolving timeline before writing or podcasting about it.
    → Supports the proposition that the wiki delivered concrete productivity value (p1) and grounds the argument that external memory beats reconstructing context from scratch under a fast-moving, information-dense story.
  • The wiki's auto-generated 'AI and Congress' concept page surfaced a cluster of recent stories Newton had half-forgotten, which he then pitched as a podcast segment.
    → Supports the proposition that the wiki automates the story-idea-generation function his old 'blips' system used to serve (p3).
  • The abandoned Capacities 'blips' system: Newton built a randomized-blip widget into his daily journal template to remind himself of neglected blips, which worked for a while but ultimately failed as the number of blips grew beyond what he could consistently track.
    → Illustrates why a purely manual, willpower-dependent knowledge-tracking system doesn't scale, motivating the shift to an LLM-automated replacement and grounding the implicit premise that discipline-dependent systems degrade as scope grows (relevant to p5, p8).
  • Newton seeded the wiki with his entire Platformer archive (since 2020), producing pages like a 12,000-word Meta page with 1,300 internal links, and found himself losing 'more than one afternoon' just browsing it the way he'd browse Wikipedia.
    → Illustrates unexpected personal/reflective value beyond narrow lookup utility, and supports the claim (p1) that the wiki's value to him is deep and idiosyncratic rather than generically demonstrable.

Questions addressed

  • ✅ Is an LLM-compiled personal wiki actually more useful in practice than existing knowledge-management apps for someone who needs to track recurring topics across years of work?
    Yes, for Newton specifically — he states it has been 'easily the most useful' productivity change of his year, superseding Notion's agentic search and his manual Capacities blips system.
  • ✅ How does a user keep a large personal LLM-maintained knowledge base honest — free of confidently-stated errors — once it becomes a working reference used in professional output?
    Partially: Newton describes always reopening original source documents before writing or speaking to confirm nothing he's about to say was hallucinated, rather than trusting the wiki's summaries outright.
  • ⭕ Should Newton, or readers generally, adopt this LLM-wiki system?
    He explicitly declines to give a confident recommendation, calling it 'too specific... and too clunky' despite it being personally his most valuable change of the year.
  • ⭕ As the wiki's scope keeps growing (more pages, longer pages, more source material added daily), does its maintenance cost eventually outpace the value it returns?
